# China distributor exclusivity: what to give, and what to keep

Give a China distributor exclusivity one region and one channel at a time.
Tie each grant to a yearly purchase target and a review date. Keep your
online stores out of it, along with the right to take the grant back.
Chinese courts hold both sides to an exclusive deal, so write it narrowly
and end it in writing.

*Court filings and the rules quoted here checked October 12, 2026.*

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

In our experience, the first contract a Chinese distributor sends over asks
for the lot: the whole country, every channel, every product, for five years or
more. That's an opening position. Treat it as one.

Exclusivity is a fair thing to give. A distributor that buys your stock and
spends a year getting it onto shelves wants to know you won't hand the same
market to a rival once the work is done. The work is deciding how much of it
to give, and what you want back for it.

This is an operator's guide. The rules quoted here were checked against the
official texts in October 2026, and the contract itself should be drafted
and read by a lawyer qualified in China before you sign.

<!-- SECTION: Why distributors ask for everything -->

## Why distributors ask for everything

They carry the money. A distributor buys your goods and holds the stock
until it sells. When the market softens, the distributor feels it first.

> One distributor put a year in numbers: four to five million RMB spent on
> stock, for less than 300,000 RMB of actual profit.
> Source: Jiemian (界面新闻), carrying Kuaidao Caijing (快刀财经), July 2025.
> https://www.jiemian.com/article/13003026.html

Margins that thin leave no room for a second distributor in the same city
cutting price. So the ask for national, all-channel exclusivity is partly
defense. Part of it is a land grab, too. A distributor that holds the whole
country can appoint its own sub-distributors in the provinces it doesn't
reach, and sell to them at a markup.

What the brand gives up is harder to see on signing day. Every region and
channel inside the grant is one you can't sell into yourself, or hand to
anyone else, until the contract ends or you end it properly. A Beijing case
shows what "properly" means.

<!-- SECTION: The Beijing case -->

## Courts enforce exclusivity both ways

In March 2008 Dirui, a urinalysis equipment maker from Changchun, made
Beijing Cheng'antang its exclusive distributor for the city. Targets were to
be agreed every January.

> The grant covered all Dirui urinalysis products in Beijing for five years.
> The 2008 target was 32,000 tubes of test strips and 8 H800 analyzers; the
> 2009 target was 34,000 tubes and 10 analyzers. After two straight years of
> missed targets, Dirui canceled the exclusivity from 2010, treated
> Cheng'antang as an ordinary distributor and began supplying other Beijing
> distributors and hospitals.
> Source: Dirui Medical (迪瑞医疗), IPO prospectus, August 2014.
> http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2014-08-28/1200180236.PDF

The missed targets were real. What Dirui's filings don't show is a written
termination. Dirui only went to court to end the contract in July 2012,
after two years of supplying other distributors in Beijing.

> Cheng'antang claimed 7,508,040 RMB in damages through the end of 2012. On
> appeal, the Beijing No. 1 Intermediate People's Court ordered Dirui to pay
> it 5,864,950 RMB in liquidated damages and dismissed Dirui's counterclaim.
> The judgment was final.
> Source: Dirui Medical (迪瑞医疗), announcement on litigation progress,
> April 2016. http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2016-04-16/1202187573.PDF

Dirui paid about 78% of what Cheng'antang asked for, to a distributor that
had missed its targets two years running.

The judgment's full reasoning isn't public outside China's court database,
so read the lesson from the facts. A missed target gave Dirui a reason to
act, and the grant stood until Dirui ended it properly, which the filings
never show it doing. Our guide to
[replacing a China distributor](/insights/replace-distributor-china) walks
through the switch Dirui should have run.

The same logic protects brands. A Shanghai court held a distributor to the
channel limits in its own exclusive grant.

> In February 2024 a Chinese cosmetics brand gave a company exclusive
> regional sales rights, limited it and its retailers to named offline
> stores, and set a 200,000 RMB penalty for selling across channels. The
> goods turned up in online shops with their traceability codes scratched
> off. In June 2025 the Fengxian court ruled that buying from other channels
> and selling online was diversion under the contract. Fourteen similar
> cases then closed through mediation, with more than 6 million RMB at
> stake.
> Source: People's Court Daily (人民法院报), via the Supreme People's Court
> (最高人民法院), August 2026.
> https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/507751.html

The judge in the Shanghai case named loose wording on what counts as
diversion as one cause of the dispute. Both cases point the same way. An
exclusive grant is enforced as written, against whichever side breaks it.

## Five ways to grant China distributor exclusivity

Split the grant into pieces and price each piece on its own. Here is what
each one costs you, and what to ask for it.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Grant type | What the brand gives up | Target to attach | Exit trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| National | Every other distributor in mainland China, plus any direct sales the contract doesn't carve out | A yearly purchase volume, plus named provinces covered by a set date | A missed year or an uncovered province turns the grant non-exclusive on written notice |
| Regional (a province or city) | Other distributors in that region. Dirui's Beijing grant covered all its urinalysis products for five years | A volume agreed before each year starts (Dirui, 2008: 32,000 tubes and 8 analyzers) | The same, region by region. With no deadline written, act within one year of the miss (Civil Code Art. 564) |
| Channel (supermarkets, pharmacies, foodservice) | Other distributors in that channel | A volume per channel, plus a store listing count you can check | Selling outside the channel triggers the agreed penalty (Fengxian: 200,000 RMB, offline-only grant) |
| Online | Your flagship store, if the grant includes it. Tmall and Douyin let a partner open it only under your exclusive authorization | Yearly sales per platform, read in a store backend you can log into | A missed target ends the online grant, and the authorization letter is dated to lapse with it |
| Category (product lines) | Other partners for the lines in the grant | A volume per line. New launches stay outside unless added in writing | A line below target leaves the grant on notice; the other lines stay |

</div>

*Sources: Dirui IPO prospectus (August 2014), Civil Code Art. 564, People's
Court Daily (August 2026), Tmall entry standard (revised June 2025), Douyin
shop help center (observed October 2026). Full citations in the sections
around the table.*

## Targets and take-or-pay

A target is what turns exclusivity into a trade. Write it in units or in
purchase value, for a calendar year, and agree the next year's number before
the year starts. Build the first one from the sales plan the distributor
pitched to win the deal; it's hard for them to call their own forecast
unrealistic. Dirui's contract left each year's target to a January
meeting. When Dirui stopped signing yearly targets in 2010, the exclusive
contract kept running anyway.

Then decide what a miss does. The most useful clause converts the grant
instead of ending the contract: below target, exclusivity falls away on
written notice and the distributor stays on as an ordinary one. That's
roughly what Dirui did in practice. Written into the contract as a right,
and sent on paper, the same move stands on firmer ground.

Take-or-pay goes a step further. The distributor commits to a floor volume
and pays a shortfall fee if it buys less. Price that fee from your lost
margin, because a Chinese court can trim it.

> A party can ask a court to cut a contract penalty that is excessively
> above the loss. A penalty more than 30% above the actual loss is
> generally treated as excessive, and a clause saying the penalty can't be
> adjusted doesn't stop the court. A party in bad-faith breach generally
> can't get its penalty reduced.
> Source: Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院), interpretation on the Civil
> Code's general contract rules, Arts. 64 and 65, December 2023.
> https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/419382.html

The Civil Code also lets the parties agree a formula in place of a lump
sum. A formula tied to the shortfall (the margin you'd have earned on the
cartons it didn't buy) is easier to defend than a round number picked at
the signing table.

> Parties can agree a fixed penalty or a method for calculating damages,
> and a court can raise a penalty below the loss or reduce one excessively
> above it (Art. 585). A deposit (定金) can't exceed 20% of the contract's
> value; anything above that doesn't count as a deposit (Art. 586).
> Source: National People's Congress, Civil Code (中华人民共和国民法典), via
> the Ministry of Commerce (商务部) policy database, in force January 2021.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=70524

Some brands also take a deposit against the first year's orders. Keep it
under that 20% line.

One thing stays out of the target clause entirely: the distributor's
selling price.

> A supplier may not agree with a distributor to fix the price it resells
> at, or to set a minimum resale price, unless the supplier can prove the
> deal doesn't restrict competition or that its market share sits below the
> regulator's threshold.
> Source: Anti-Monopoly Law (中华人民共和国反垄断法), Art. 18, in force
> August 2022, via the Ministry of Commerce (商务部) policy database.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=97047

Set the target in volume and leave the resale price alone.

<!-- SECTION: Online carve-outs -->

## Online carve-outs: keep the flagship

Online is where a regional grant leaks. A shopper in Chengdu can buy from a
store run out of Shanghai, and the platforms' own rules don't split stores
by region.

> A Tmall flagship store sells the brand owner's own brand, or a brand held
> under the trademark owner's exclusive authorization. A specialty store
> sells under an ordinary authorization, which may not carry a regional
> limit (some categories excepted) and must run at least six months.
> Source: Tmall (天猫), entry standard (天猫入驻标准), revised June 2025.
> https://rulechannel.tmall.com/?type=detail&ruleId=9257&cId=378#/rule/detail?ruleId=9257&cId=378

> On Douyin, a flagship that sells a brand the store's owner doesn't own
> also needs an exclusive authorization.
> Source: Douyin shop help center (抖店官网), store types, observed October
> 2026. https://fxg.jinritemai.com/question/type

Two things follow. An exclusive authorization can only go to one party, so
a distributor with online rights may ask for the letter that opens your
flagship. And a regional distributor with a Tmall specialty store sells to
the whole country, because its authorization can't be drawn around a
province.

So keep online as its own grant. The cleanest version leaves the flagship
with you, run by your own team or a Tmall Partner, while the distributor
supplies offline. If a distributor really is your best online partner, give
online rights separately, with their own target and an authorization letter
that ends with the grant.

Running both side by side takes price discipline. When we took over
DaVinci Gourmet's China e-commerce, its Tmall and JD flagships sat
alongside a distributor network. Once pricing was aligned between Tmall
and the distributors, cross-channel pricing disputes were resolved
within three months, as the [DaVinci Gourmet case](/work/kerry-davinci)
records.

Splitting the grant by channel in the first year is how we set up
[distribution in China](/enter-china/distribution), with national
exclusivity held back until a distributor has hit hard volume numbers.

<!-- SECTION: Notice and review -->

## How to take a grant back: notice and a review date

Put a review date in the contract, once a year, a month or two after the
year's sales are counted. At the review the brand can narrow the grant if
the target was missed: take a region or a channel out of it, or turn it
non-exclusive.

Then do it on paper.

> A party ending a contract must notify the other side, and the contract
> ends when the notice arrives. Where no deadline is set, the right to
> terminate lapses if it isn't used within one year of the day the holder
> knew, or should have known, the grounds. An open-ended contract for
> continuing supply can be ended at any time, on reasonable prior notice.
> Source: National People's Congress, Civil Code (中华人民共和国民法典),
> Arts. 563 to 565, via the Ministry of Commerce (商务部) policy database.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=70524

The one-year clock is the trap. A brand that lets a missed target ride for
eighteen months may find its right to act on that miss has gone. Write a
deadline you can live with into the contract, and act inside it.

Send the notice to the address the contract names and keep proof that it
arrived. Cite the clause you rely on, and the target that was missed.
Appoint the next distributor only after the notice has landed. Dirui did it
the other way around.

The strongest position at the table is a real alternative. A brand with
two or three credible candidates can price exclusivity properly. Our guide to
[finding a distributor in China](/compass/find-a-distributor-in-china)
shows how [Compass](/compass) builds that list.

## Questions on China distributor exclusivity

**Is exclusive distribution legal in China?**
Yes, as a contract term, and courts enforce it, as the Beijing and Shanghai
cases show. The Anti-Monopoly Law's rule on supplier and distributor deals
names resale prices (fixing them or setting a floor) and lets the regulator
add others. Keep price out of the exclusivity clause, and have a
China-qualified lawyer check the rest against your market position.

**Can I end exclusivity if the distributor misses its target?**
Only the way the contract allows, and in writing. A missed target gives you
grounds to act, and the grant stands until you do. Dirui stopped treating
its Beijing distributor as exclusive after two missed years, with no written
termination on the record, and paid 5,864,950 RMB on appeal.

**Should my distributor run my Tmall flagship?**
Only under a separate grant. Tmall and Douyin let a partner open a flagship
for a brand it doesn't own only with an exclusive authorization, so whoever
holds that letter holds your official store. Give it with its own target
and an end date, or keep the flagship with your own team or a Tmall Partner.

**How long should an exclusive grant run?**
As long as the targets are met. A shorter first term, or a yearly review
with the right to narrow the grant, protects a distributor that performs
just as well as a long one. It also gives you a way out if it doesn't.
Renewal can be automatic in any year the targets are hit.
