# How to localize a brand for China before you sell

Localizing a brand for China starts at the trademark office. File your Latin
mark and your Chinese name there before you announce anything; online, it
costs 270 RMB a class. Then fix what Chinese law reads: the label on the
pack, the product pages, ad copy stripped of "best" and a seven-day return
policy.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Updated October 1, 2026. The February 2023 version of this page was a
seven-point checklist with no figures and no sources. This one puts the steps
in the order Chinese law and the platforms enforce them. It adds the official
fees and the cross-border exemption, plus two court cases that show what a
late filing costs.

| Store build and launch content | Chinese store pages and product detail | Tmall Global, JD Worldwide, Douyin | 25,000 RMB in our calculators |

Only the first row is something a competitor can take from you.

## Register the name before anyone sees it

China's trademark system rewards whoever files first. If two companies file
the same or a similar mark for the same goods, the earlier application goes
through, even if the other company sold under the name for years.

> When two applicants file identical or similar marks for the same or similar
> goods, the trademark office approves the earlier application; a same-day tie
> goes to the earlier user.
> Source: China National Intellectual Property Administration (国家知识产权局),
> Trademark Law (2019 amendment), article 31, July 2019.
> https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2019/7/30/art_95_28179.html

Filing is cheap. The official fee covers one class and ten goods or services.

> An online trademark application costs 270 RMB per class for up to ten
> items, plus 27 RMB for each extra item. A paper filing costs 300 RMB and
> 30 RMB.
> Source: CNIPA Trademark Office (国家知识产权局商标局), fee list in force since
> July 1, 2019. https://sbj.cnipa.gov.cn/sbj/sbsq/sfbz/

So a brand that files its Latin mark and its Chinese name in, say, three
classes pays 1,620 RMB in official fees. Agent fees come on top. Then it waits.

> The average examination period for a trademark registration has held at
> four months.
> Source: CNIPA, 2026 national work report, January 2026.
> https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/1/7/art_53_203564.html

New Balance found out what waiting costs. It sold in China under the name
新百伦. A Chinese businessman, Zhou Lerong, already owned the registered marks
百伦 and 新百伦, the second approved in January 2008, and sued.

> A Guangzhou court ordered New Balance to pay 98 million RMB, half its
> estimated profit from the name. The Guangdong High People's Court cut the
> award to 5 million RMB on June 23, 2016.
> Source: Yangcheng Evening News (羊城晚报), carried by People's Daily Online
> (人民网), June 2016.
> http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2016/0624/c1004-28477265.html

Michael Jordan was on the other side of the same problem, and spent four
years on it. He began challenging Qiaodan Sports, a Chinese sporting goods
company, in 2012. In December 2016 China's Supreme People's Court ruled that
its registration of 乔丹, the characters for "Jordan," violated his prior
right to his name. The pinyin version, QIAODAN, stayed with the company.

> The court ordered the Chinese-character 乔丹 marks re-examined because they
> harmed Jordan's prior name right, and upheld the seven cases over the pinyin
> QIAODAN, in which he held no name right.
> Source: China National Radio (央广网), December 2016.
> http://china.cnr.cn/xwwgf/20161208/t20161208_523312598.shtml

The lesson is narrow. Chinese shoppers search for the characters and say
them out loud. Own them, and own the Latin spelling, in every class you sell
in.

The 2026 revision of the law helps a little. From January 1, 2027, it spells
out that filings not meant for use, and far beyond normal business needs, are
refused, and it puts a number on the fine for bad-faith filers: up to 100,000
RMB. The window to oppose someone else's published mark also shrinks, from
three months to two. Somebody on your side has to watch the gazette.

> The revised law keeps the first-to-file rule word for word as Article 35,
> refuses registration where a filing is not made for use and clearly exceeds
> normal business needs (Article 19), fines bad-faith filers up to 100,000 RMB
> (Article 54) and takes effect January 1, 2027.
> Source: CNIPA, Trademark Law (2026 revision), June 2026.
> https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2026/6/26/art_95_206942.html

## Pick a Chinese name shoppers can say

A foreign name reaches Chinese through its sound, its meaning, or some of
each. Coca-Cola's 可口可乐 (roughly "tasty and fun") does both. A name built only on sound can land on
characters that read badly, or on characters someone else already registered.

Reverse the order most naming projects use. Clear the trademark register
first, in your classes, for every shortlisted name. Then test the survivors
with shoppers in the city tiers you plan to sell in. That's how our
[Chinese naming and trademark screening](/enter-china/branding-localisation)
work runs. An uncleared shortlist is a list of names you might not be allowed
to use.

## Labels: where cross-border saves a step

The route you choose decides what goes on the box. Goods sold through Tmall
Global or JD Worldwide under China's cross-border retail import rules are
treated as personal imports, and they can ship in their home-market
packaging.

> Cross-border retail imports are supervised as goods for personal use, and
> first-import permits, registration and filing requirements do not apply.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce (商务部) and five other bodies, notice
> 商财发〔2018〕486号, November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

The exemption has a condition. The seller and the platform must warn the
buyer that the goods may carry no Chinese label, and show a Chinese
electronic label on the product page. So the translation still gets done.
It lives online.

> The risk notice must state that the goods are bought directly from
> overseas, may have no Chinese label and that the consumer can view the
> product's Chinese electronic label on the website.
> Source: MOFCOM and five other bodies, 商财发〔2018〕486号, article 4,
> November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

General trade is different. Once a distributor or your own China entity
imports the goods and sells them inside the country, the Chinese label goes
on the pack. The Product Quality Law asks for the product name and the
maker's name and address in Chinese, and category rules add more. Cosmetics
need the full ingredient list, the filing number and the domestic
responsible person, for a start.

> Product labels must carry the product name and the maker's name and
> address in Chinese.
> Source: CNIPA, repost of the Product Quality Law, article 27, July 2019.
> https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2019/7/31/art_104_67810.html

> Cosmetics sold in China must carry a Chinese label showing the Chinese
> product name, the registration or filing details, the full ingredient list,
> net content and use-by date, and for an overseas registrant the name and
> address of its domestic responsible person.
> Source: National Medical Products Administration (国家药监局) announcement
> 2021 No. 77, in the State Council Gazette (国务院公报), May 2021.
> https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5631831.htm

It's one reason to test China cross-border first and move to a
[distributor under general trade](/enter-china/distribution) once a product
proves itself. The relabeling bill waits until the volume justifies it. Our
[cross-border setup](/enter-china/cross-border-setup) work covers the first
route. Start the label file for the second one early anyway.

## Copy the Advertising Law will read

Global taglines are where this goes wrong. "The world's best
serum." "No. 1 in Europe." Translated faithfully, both break Chinese
advertising law. SAMR's own guideline applies the rule to a seller's own
website as well as to paid media.

> Advertisements may not use terms such as "national-level," "highest-level"
> or "best." Advertisers who do face fines of 200,000 to 1,000,000 RMB, and
> in serious cases the loss of their business license.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (国家市场监督管理总局),
> Advertising Law as amended April 2021, articles 9 and 57.
> https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_5474cf75173c45d6a0379730fb4e8d97.html

There's some room for a first mistake. SAMR's 2023 enforcement guideline lets
regulators waive the penalty when a first use does little harm and is fixed
quickly. The same guideline says ads for health food, drugs and medical
devices generally don't count as minor, so supplement and device brands
should expect no grace period.

> A first use of an absolute term that causes minor harm and is promptly
> corrected may go unpunished; ads for health food, drugs and medical devices
> are generally not treated as minor violations.
> Source: SAMR, Guidelines on Enforcement Against Absolute Terms in
> Advertising, announcement No. 6, February 2023.
> https://www.samr.gov.cn/ggjgs/tzgg/art/2023/art_183b5cb48d9e4f0dba67f9f912a913ba.html

These fines get issued. In April 2024 a market regulator in Anyang, Henan,
fined a company 109,600 RMB for an online cosmetics ad that claimed to be
first in sales across the whole internet.

> The ad used absolute terms such as "first in sales, nationwide, across the
> whole internet," and the claims did not match the facts. The Anyang market
> regulator fined the company 109,600 RMB in April 2024.
> Source: SAMR release, carried by the Shanghai Medical Products
> Administration (上海市药品监督管理局), January 2025.
> https://yjj.sh.gov.cn/zjyw/20250117/e9ebc3d76a4146d2aa2cc5b2a5035918.html

Brief your translator on this list before the first product page goes up.
Ranking claims need proof you can hand to a regulator, with a source and a
date.

## Store pages and the seven-day rule

Chinese shoppers expect a store that works like the domestic stores they
already use, down to Chinese-speaking service in the platform chat. The
return policy is set by law.

> Consumers who buy online may return goods within seven days of receipt
> without giving a reason. Custom-made goods, fresh and perishable goods,
> unsealed digital products and delivered newspapers are excluded.
> Source: SAMR, Consumer Rights Protection Law, article 25, as amended
> October 2013.
> https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_0a323e046fba43f0b6e9e977f1e8d5fc.html

For the store build, our three calculators each carry one localization line: 25,000 RMB for store setup and launch content on
Tmall Global and JD Worldwide, and the same figure as a brand setup
contribution on Douyin, written off over three years. The trademark line in
all three defaults to zero (TheChinaPath calculator data, October 2026). The
Tmall Global model also assumes a registered mark: its 50,000 RMB deposit
baseline is for a flagship store with a registered (R) mark, and a pending
(TM) application raises it.

That zero is an assumption, and it's ours. The model expects you to own your
Chinese marks before the store opens, since a store selling under a name
another company registered can be ordered to stop, and the reviews and search
history it built under that name stay behind when it does. If you don't own
the marks yet, put the filing in. Change both inputs in the
[Tmall Global setup and run calculator](/tools/tmall-global-setup-and-run)
and watch the cost per order move.

It barely moves. At the official fee, two marks in three classes add about
540 RMB a year over the three-year write-off.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to register a trademark in China?

Examination averages about four months, according to CNIPA. A mark that
passes is then published, and other parties have three months to oppose it
under the current law. From January 1, 2027, the revised Trademark Law
shortens that window to two months. File early, because the first filer
wins and nothing you do in another country reserves the name in China.

### Do cross-border products need a Chinese label?

Not on the pack. Goods sold under China's cross-border retail import rules,
on Tmall Global or JD Worldwide, can ship in their original packaging. The
seller and the platform must warn the buyer the goods may have no Chinese
label and show a Chinese electronic label online. Goods sold through a
distributor under general trade need the Chinese label on the package.

### Can I say "best" in Chinese advertising?

No. The Advertising Law bans terms such as "national-level," "highest-level"
and "best," with fines of 200,000 to 1,000,000 RMB. SAMR may waive a first,
minor, quickly fixed breach, though health food, drugs and medical devices
are generally not treated as minor. Ranking claims need evidence you can
show a regulator, with a source and a date.

### What does localization cost before the first sale?

The official trademark fee is 270 RMB per class online, so two marks in three
classes is 1,620 RMB before agent fees. Our calculators budget 25,000 RMB for
store setup and launch content. Naming research, packaging and translation
depend on the range and are scoped as a project after a first call.
