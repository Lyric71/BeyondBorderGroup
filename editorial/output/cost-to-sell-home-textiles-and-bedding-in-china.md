# What it costs to sell bedding and linens in China

Start on JD Worldwide. A bedding brand opens there on a US$5,000 flagship
deposit, pays no annual fee and gives up 3.9% of each sale. Tmall Global asks
50,000 RMB, a 60,000 RMB annual fee and 5%, though new stores pay at most half
until April 2027. Douyin opens from 500 RMB and takes 6%. Then keep every
item under 5,000 RMB.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

The cost to sell bedding in China is mostly a percentage. That sets this
category apart from food, where a 15 RMB parcel eats the margin on a small
basket. A duvet set sells for hundreds or thousands of yuan, so the
commission line outgrows the courier bill fast.

Bedding also runs into a ceiling most cross-border categories never reach.
The 5,000 RMB single-transaction limit sits right inside the price band of a
premium imported duvet.

## The cost to sell bedding in China, platform by platform

<div class="table-scroll">

| Bedding, first year (RMB unless stated) | Tmall Global | JD Worldwide | Douyin cross-border |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security deposit, refundable | 50,000 | US$5,000 (about 35,500) | 500 to 5,000 |
| Annual platform fee | 60,000 (30,000 or none for stores opening by March 2027) | none | none |
| Commission on each sale | 5% | 3% plus a 0.9% transaction fee | 6%, plus 4% on Douyin Mall and Doubao orders |
| Cash in before the first sale | 50,000 to 110,000 | about 35,500 | 500 to 5,000 |

</div>

Rates as posted on each platform's rule pages, checked September 30, 2026.

Tmall Global is the priciest door.

> Tmall Global charges bedding (床上用品) a 5% technical service fee and a
> 60,000 RMB annual fee, under the category schedule in force from
> September 9, 2026.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), category annual fee and technical service
> fee rates, September 2026.
> https://rule.tmall.hk/?type=detail&ruleId=11005909&cId=625

> The home category deposit is 50,000 RMB for a flagship, specialty or
> multi-brand store, and 100,000 RMB for a brand that holds only a pending
> TM application.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), platform deposit rules, May 2026.
> https://rule.tmall.hk/?type=detail&ruleId=8942&cId=625

JD quotes in dollars and scales the deposit by store type and sales.

> JD Worldwide charges bedding a 3% operating support fee plus a 0.9%
> transaction fee, and asks US$5,000 from a brand flagship. Other store
> types pay US$10,000 to US$15,000.
> Source: JD Worldwide (京东全球购), category fee standard, effective
> August 1, 2026.
> https://jdw-rule.jd.hk/detail?ruleId=950583665543483392

Douyin is the odd one. It barely asks for a deposit, then takes the biggest
cut: 10% on orders that come through its Mall app or Doubao, ByteDance's AI
assistant.

> Douyin's cross-border stores pay 6% on home goods (居家日用), the group
> that holds bedding, plus a 4% supplement on orders placed through the
> Douyin Mall app and Doubao.
> Source: Douyin E-commerce (抖音电商), cross-border technical service fee
> standard, July 2026.
> https://school.jinritemai.com/doudian/web/articlev0/aHMYyFoqESKn

> The cross-border base deposit is 500, 2,000 or 5,000 RMB, set each month
> by the store's paid GMV the month before.
> Source: Douyin E-commerce (抖音电商), cross-border deposit rules, June 2025.
> https://school.jinritemai.com/doudian/web/articlev0/107821

JD is cheaper on every line. Tmall softens the annual fee for stores that
open before March 31, 2027: 30,000 RMB instead of 60,000, or nothing at all
for the home categories on a separate waiver list. Tmall doesn't publish that
list, so ask the category manager before you budget. From April 2027 the full
fee comes back.

> Merchants in the 60,000 RMB annual fee tier that open between April 1,
> 2026 and March 31, 2027, or opened in the year before, pay 30,000 RMB for
> that period.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), 2026 annual fee incentive for new and
> second-year merchants, July 2026.
> https://rule.tmall.hk/?type=detail&ruleId=20010439&cId=625

Chinese bedding buyers are on JD, too. Fuanna, one of the big listed bedding
makers, took 41.9% of its 2025 online sales there and 28.3% on Tmall. One
company's numbers, granted.

> Fuanna's online channels made about 44.38% of 2025 revenue. JD accounted
> for 41.87% of online sales, Tmall for 28.30%.
> Source: Shenzhen Fuanna Bedding and Furnishing (富安娜), 2025 annual
> report, April 2026.
> http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-25/1225189767.PDF

## Bedding clears cross-border, up to 5,000 RMB an item

Start with the list. Blankets, bed linen, towels and down-filled bedding all
sit on the positive list of goods the bonded route accepts.

> The cross-border retail import list (2019 edition) covers blankets,
> printed and other bed linen of cotton and man-made fibers, bath towels
> and down-filled bedding.
> Source: Ministry of Finance (财政部) and 12 other departments,
> Announcement 2019 No. 96, December 2019.
> https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/201912/t20191227_3451448.htm

Inside the limits the shopper pays 9.1%: zero duty, and 70% of the 13%
import VAT. Bedding carries no consumption tax.

> The cross-border retail import tariff rate is set at 0%, and import VAT
> and consumption tax are levied at 70% of the statutory payable amount.
> Source: Ministry of Finance (财政部), 财关税〔2016〕18号, March 2016.
> http://www.mof.gov.cn/gp/xxgkml/gss/201603/t20160324_2510682.htm

Now the ceiling. A single transaction tops out at 5,000 RMB. One item priced
above that can still come in cross-border, but the tax is charged in full at
general rates. Two items over the line in one order can't use the route at
all.

> Where the dutiable price exceeds the 5,000 RMB single-transaction limit
> but stays under the 26,000 RMB annual limit, and the order holds only one
> item, it may enter through cross-border retail with duty, import VAT and
> consumption tax charged in full.
> Source: Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs and State
> Taxation Administration, 财关税〔2018〕49号, November 2018.
> http://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/201811/t20181129_3079073.htm

> The 2026 most-favored-nation duty is 6% on cotton bed sheets and 10% on
> down or feather-filled quilts.
> Source: State Council Tariff Commission (国务院关税税则委员会), Customs
> Tariff of the PRC (2026), Announcement 2025 No. 12, December 2025, as
> published by the Wuxi Finance Bureau (无锡市财政局).
> https://cz.wuxi.gov.cn/doc/2026/01/04/4711263.shtml

Run that on a 5,200 RMB down duvet. Duty is 520 RMB, and 13% VAT on the
price plus duty adds another 744. The shopper pays 1,264 RMB in tax, about
24%. At 4,990 RMB the same duvet would carry 454, so a 210 RMB price cut is
worth 810 RMB of tax to the shopper.

Labels split by route. Cross-border goods ship in their original packaging,
and the order page tells the shopper they may carry no Chinese label, with a
Chinese electronic label online instead.

> Goods are bought directly from overseas and may have no Chinese label;
> consumers can view the Chinese electronic label on the website.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce (商务部) and five other bodies,
> 商财发〔2018〕486号, article 4, November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

A range that lives above the limit ends up in general trade, sold through a
Chinese importer into domestic stores. That brings in China's mandatory
textile safety code, GB 18401. It
sorts products into baby goods, goods that touch skin and everything else,
and grades them A, B and C. Sheets and pillowcases touch skin. A crib set is
baby goods, held to the strictest formaldehyde limit.

> GB 18401-2010 groups textiles as infant products, skin-contact products
> and non-skin-contact products, graded A, B and C, with formaldehyde capped
> at 20, 75 and 300 mg/kg.
> Source: China Quality News (中国质量新闻网), March 2014.
> https://m.cqn.com.cn/xfzn/content/2014-03/21/content_2131036.htm

> GB 18401-2010 is a mandatory national standard, in force since August 1,
> 2011, and was confirmed on review in April 2022.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (市场监管总局), national
> standards register, record reviewed April 2022.
> https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=71F772D7D9F6D3A7E05397BE0A0AB82A

The pack also needs Chinese before it reaches a shop.

> Product labels must give the product name and the maker's name and
> address in Chinese, plus specification, grade and main components where
> the product calls for them.
> Source: Product Quality Law (产品质量法), article 27, as amended December
> 2018, published by the China National Intellectual Property
> Administration (国家知识产权局), July 2019.
> https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2019/7/31/art_104_67810.html

Down gets tested once it's on a Chinese shelf. Jiangsu's market regulator
failed 6 of 63 down quilts in its 2025 check, most of them on how much down
was actually inside.

> Of 63 batches of down quilts sampled in Jiangsu, 6 failed, on down
> content, fiber content and goose or duck down share.
> Source: Jiangsu Administration for Market Regulation (江苏省市场监督管理局),
> January 2026.
> https://scjgj.jiangsu.gov.cn/art/2026/1/4/art_78969_11705277.html

## What one bedding order actually costs

We couldn't find an average bedding basket in any filing or trade report. The
price bands are public, though. They're high.

> LOVO, Luolai's online-only line, sells at 300 to 2,000 RMB. The Australian
> and Italian bedding labels Luolai licenses, Sheridan and Zucchi, list 5,000
> to 25,000. Luolai reported a 17.70% return rate on Tmall for 2025.
> Source: Luolai Lifestyle Technology (罗莱生活), 2025 annual report, April
> 2026.
> http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-23/1225148171.PDF

> Fuanna's main label sells at 2,000 to 6,000 RMB. It reported a 9.80%
> return rate on Tmall for 2025, and 10.17% on JD.
> Source: Fuanna (富安娜), 2025 annual report, April 2026.
> http://static.cninfo.com.cn/finalpage/2026-04-25/1225189767.PDF

That's two to three times the 5% our own calculators assume by default. On
the cross-border route a return at least doesn't cost you the tax.

> Returns applied for within 30 days of release, with the goods back at the
> original bonded site within 45 days, have the tax not collected.
> Source: General Administration of Customs (海关总署), Announcement 2020
> No. 45, March 2020.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-03/28/content_5496571.htm

Here's what Fuanna's 9.8% does to a store. Bear with the arithmetic; it's what
picks the platform.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Basket (RMB) | Tmall Global, cost per kept order (RMB) | Share of the order | JD Worldwide, cost per kept order (RMB) | Share of the order | Tax the shopper pays (RMB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 400 duvet cover | 44 | 10.9% | 35 | 8.8% | 36 |
| 1,500 bedding set | 110 | 7.3% | 78 | 5.2% | 137 |
| 4,800 down duvet | 308 | 6.4% | 207 | 4.3% | 437 |

</div>

> Cost per kept order at a 9.8% return rate, from the per-parcel defaults in
> our Tmall Global and JD Worldwide models (4 RMB pick and pack, 11 RMB last
> mile, 15 RMB return handling) and the platform rates above.
> Source: TheChinaPath calculator data, September 2026.

On a 1,500 RMB set, commission and payment fees make up 82% of the Tmall
store cost. The parcel barely registers. So the rate matters more here than
in a food or beauty store. Tmall takes 6% with the Alipay fee, JD takes 3.9%,
and the 2.1-point gap is worth about 31 RMB on every kept order. A thousand
orders in, it has paid for Tmall's discounted annual fee.

One caveat on the parcel line. The 15 RMB default was set for a shoebox. A
boxed king duvet costs more to move, so treat it as the floor.

Breakeven next, at the 1,500 RMB set and a 50% gross margin. Each kept order
contributes about 640 RMB on Tmall.

The fixed side is mostly people. If an operating partner runs the store for
you (on Tmall, a Tmall Partner), our calculator loads 35,000 RMB a month for
one by default. Add the 30,000 RMB new-merchant
annual fee and a 25,000 RMB store build written off over three years, and
you need about 720 kept orders. That's roughly 1.07 million RMB of year-one
sales, near US$151,000, before advertising. On JD the same store needs about
640 orders.

In cash, that fixed side comes to about 475,000 RMB for year one on Tmall and
445,000 on JD, before stock, ads and the refundable deposit. Nearly all of it
is the partner.

## Which platform first

JD Worldwide. It's cheaper to open and cheaper per order. Chinese bedding
buyers are there already.

Two things flip it. If your range lives above 5,000 RMB an item, the
cross-border tax advantage is gone, and a sets-and-pillows order over the
line can't ship that way at all. Go general trade with a Chinese partner,
meet GB 18401 and sell on a domestic store. And if you can shoot video and
have someone to host it, Douyin opens on a 500 RMB deposit. At 6% to 10% a
sale you wouldn't want to scale there first. As a test of whether Chinese
shoppers want your sheets, it costs next to nothing to set up.

## Questions we get asked

**Can I sell a duvet priced above 5,000 RMB cross-border?**

Yes, if it ships alone. A single item over the 5,000 RMB limit can still
clear as a cross-border order, but the shopper pays full duty and full VAT,
about 24% on a down duvet instead of 9.1%. The order also counts toward the
shopper's 26,000 RMB annual quota. Two or more items worth over 5,000 RMB
together can't use the route.

**Does imported bedding need a Chinese label?**

Not on the bonded cross-border route. The order page carries a risk notice
and a Chinese electronic label instead. Under general trade the goods have to
meet the mandatory safety code GB 18401, which holds baby bedding to the
strictest formaldehyde limit, and the pack needs a Chinese label before it
reaches a shop.

**What return rate should I plan for?**

Plan for at least 10%. Two listed Chinese bedding brands reported 9.8% and
17.7% on Tmall for 2025, against the 5% our calculators assume by default.
Returned cross-border goods back in the bonded zone within 45 days of release
have their tax cancelled. What you lose is the freight and the handling.
