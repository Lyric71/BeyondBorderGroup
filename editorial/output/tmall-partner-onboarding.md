# Tmall Partner onboarding: your first 90 days

Tmall Partner onboarding should give you a store audit, a price and assortment
plan, a campaign calendar, and a weekly report within the first 90 days. Agree
on delivery dates before work starts. If those documents are still missing at
the end of month 2, despite your supplying the agreed inputs, require a
written recovery plan.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

A Tmall Partner, or TP, operates a store for a brand. This plan assumes you’ve
hired an operator; a partner buying your stock needs a separate purchase
agreement. The first review can feel productive when the team presents revised
product pages and a sales forecast. You still need to know what the team can
do without asking you, and what must wait for your approval.

The schedule below is our recommended management plan. It isn’t a Tmall
approval deadline or a promise of sales. For a new Tmall Global store, the
cross-border marketplace for overseas merchants, distinguish the partner’s
work from platform review and the arrival of saleable stock. For an existing
store, start with its trading record and the outgoing operator’s handover.

<!-- SECTION: Onboarding schedule -->

## A Tmall Partner onboarding schedule you can sign off

Count day 1 from the agreed kickoff and record the inputs due from each side.
Put the person who accepts each deliverable beside the person who produces it.
A deadline with no named approver is easy to miss across time zones. Save
approvals with the version of the document reviewed.

The contract should identify the daily contact and their backup, plus when the
monthly retainer starts. Specify the work covered while the store awaits
approval. Weekly status reports start at kickoff; trading data follows once
sales begin.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Week range | What the TP delivers | What the brand provides | Sign it is on track |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Store audit, access register, weekly status report | Merchant documents, account access, past sales, stock records | Brand can inspect the store; gaps have owners |
| Weeks 3–4 | Price and assortment plan; launch budget | Product costs, approved claims, price limits, stock commitments | Each product variant has a price and a stock decision |
| Weeks 5–6 | Campaign calendar and order-flow checks | Budget approval and confirmed warehouse availability | Offer, checkout, dispatch, and refund routes have been checked |
| Weeks 7–8 | Trading review and corrective actions | Decisions on discounts and replenishment | Weekly report reconciles sales with returns and cash |
| Weeks 9–12; review by day 90 in week 13 | Next-quarter plan and performance review | Decision on scope and spending | Both sides can explain the results and the next spending request |

</div>

While the platform reviews the application, the team can finish an audit of
its launch preparation and the price file. Put the external dependency in the
report with the evidence of submission and the date of the latest response.

<!-- SECTION: Store control -->

## Start with access and an audit you can inspect

The access register belongs on the brand’s side. It should name the merchant
account and its recovery contacts, with payment control recorded separately
from the authority to approve advertising spend. During kickoff, have the TP
demonstrate how your team can log in to inspect the account and its
permissions (a screenshot of a login page proves very little).

The account holder carries the risk of actions taken through the store. The
platform terms below were checked on October 23, 2026.

> Tmall Global’s merchant agreement makes the merchant responsible for
> activity through its account and requires prior written consent to
> transfer the account or authorize another person to use it. The agreement
> also restricts reuse and disclosure of platform data and sets conditions
> for handling personal information.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), Merchant Service Agreement, May 2026.
> https://terms.alicdn.com/legal-agreement/terms/b_platform_service_agreement/20231027155750546/20231027155750546.html

Use the permissioned access the store supports and confirm the relevant
authorization with the platform. Sharing the main password leaves the brand
with little control over who can act. Ask the account administrator to show
how a departing team member’s access is removed.

> Taobao’s sub-account agreement allows permissioned access for
> third-party service providers, account disabling, and operation-log
> queries. Available permissions depend on the product and store.
> Source: Taobao (淘宝), Sub-account Software License Agreement, June 2023.
> https://terms.alicdn.com/legal-agreement/terms/suit_bu1_tmall/suit_bu1_tmall201802012028_72666.html

An existing store’s audit should show where the opening stock and recent sales
figures came from. Keep unresolved customer cases beside the person
responsible for closing them. Check listing problems and unused advertising
balances separately. For a new store, replace historical trading figures with
a readiness audit of the files and approvals needed to launch.

Read the account arrangement alongside the guide to [who controls a
partner-run store](/insights/tmall-partner-store-ownership). If the registered
merchant is the TP’s company, record that fact before you treat its store
dashboard as a brand-owned asset.

<!-- SECTION: Prices and cash -->

## Approve the price file before the campaign calendar

Ask for a working spreadsheet, with a separate row for every product variant
the TP wants to sell. It should show the normal selling price and the lowest
campaign price you’ll accept. Add the costs needed to judge the offer,
including fulfillment and the expected effect of returns. The brand’s finance
lead should sign off on that calculation.

A coupon can erase the margin on a product. Have the team show the buyer’s
final payment for the proposed offer and identify who funds each promotion.

Keep the approved discount limits in that file.

Keep launch cash in the same review. The first sales report won’t explain
money already committed to getting stock ready.

> TheChinaPath calculator data, October 2026: our Tmall Global model uses
> a 12-month period for each annual budget. Its editable warehouse deposit input
> is 100,000 RMB and its tax-prepayment input is 300,000 RMB, a combined
> 400,000 RMB cash assumption before other budget lines.
> Source: TheChinaPath Tmall Global calculator, October 2026.
> https://www.thechinapath.com/tools/tmall-global-setup-and-run

Replace those inputs with the terms from your warehouse and clearance
provider. They’re budget assumptions, not compulsory charges or a quote for
our services. The calculator has no store-approval time estimate.

Use the [Tmall Global budget calculator](/tools/tmall-global-setup-and-run) to
separate cash funding from recurring spending. The brand’s finance lead should
know who receives each payment and what evidence releases it. Ask for the
terms governing any unused balance before sending the money. Set a spend cap
the TP cannot raise without approval.

<!-- SECTION: Launch tests -->

## Check the order route before paying for traffic

The campaign calendar should name the products in each offer and show the
saleable stock available to support it, excluding units already reserved or
held from sale. Put the approval deadline beside the planned start date and
name the campaign owner. An event that depends on inventory still in transit
needs a smaller backup offer or a later start.

Walk through checkout and the warehouse order flow before increasing
advertising spend. Confirm that the listed variant matches the warehouse item
and that the customer sees the delivery terms you intend to honor. Ask how a
failed dispatch reaches customer service and who authorizes the refund.

Any transaction used to check the route must follow platform rules. Don’t buy
fake sales or reviews to make the launch appear busy. Save the order-flow
evidence and the person responsible for fixing any failure.

For an existing store, keep the handover date visible in the report. Orders
placed before the new TP arrived may be refunded afterward. Give those cases
an owner so they don’t disappear between two agencies.

<!-- SECTION: Weekly reporting -->

## Read refunds and settlement alongside weekly sales

Require the report in a language your decision-maker can read. Agree on the
reporting cutoff and keep it consistent. A screenshot with a rising sales
total doesn’t tell finance what reached the settlement account.

Start with paid order value and deduct completed refunds against those orders.
Pending refund requests belong alongside the total. Keep unpaid cancellations
separate so they aren’t deducted twice. Match refunds to the period of the
original sale, then reconcile the settlement statement separately because cash
may arrive later. Retain the permitted source export with each report so
finance can check it.

Keep advertising spend separate, with the attribution window written beside
any return-on-ad-spend figure. Then show stock available for the next offer
and the decision the brand needs to make about replenishment. Set a reorder
point against the supplier’s confirmed lead time, and record who can release
that order.

Ask the TP to explain a shortfall through the store’s actual records. When a
product page draws visitors who rarely pay, inspect the offer and checkout.
Paid orders that never shipped need a different explanation: trace them
through the warehouse record. Name an owner for the correction and a date when
the report will show whether it worked.

Escalate a missed delivery date when it happens. If month 2 ends without the
agreed documents, review the delivery record. A brand that hasn’t approved its
prices has helped cause the delay. If the TP has the agreed inputs and still
cannot produce usable work, freeze discretionary increases in spend and set a
dated recovery plan under the contract. Define what evidence will close each
missed delivery and book the follow-up review before the call ends.

At day 90, decide whether the next quarter’s budget is justified. Compare the
results with the assumptions both sides approved, and separate unfinished
launch work from trading performance. If the appointment needs to change, use
the evidence you’ve collected to write a tighter scope for the next operator.

The [Tmall Partner selection guide](/compass/find-a-tmall-partner-in-china)
sets out what to check before that appointment. Keep the onboarding
deliverables in the brief you submit through [Compass](/compass).

<!-- SECTION: FAQ -->

## Questions to settle at kickoff

### Does a Tmall Partner have to launch the store in 90 days?

The schedule here is a proposed management plan, not an official launch
deadline. Agree separately on the documents the TP must deliver and the
dependencies outside its control. An approval delay should still leave you
with evidence of submission, a clear record of what is missing, and a revised
date for the next check.

### What if the new TP inherits a poorly run store?

Set a baseline before judging its results. Ask for dated exports of orders and
stock, plus the record of unresolved customer cases. Assign responsibility for
refunds tied to earlier sales. The new team should explain which problems it
can fix within its scope and where it needs brand approval or cooperation from
the outgoing operator.

### Should the brand receive every customer record?

Ask only for the evidence needed to check trading and settlement, using
aggregate or masked records where possible. Tmall Global’s agreement restricts
reuse and disclosure of platform data and sets conditions for handling
personal information. Have the TP identify the permitted reporting method for
your store before it exports customer-level records to a shared drive or
headquarters.
