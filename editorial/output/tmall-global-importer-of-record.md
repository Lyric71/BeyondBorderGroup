# Tmall Global importer of record: who fills the role

Nobody, in the sense you mean. Tmall Global has no Chinese importer of
record: your overseas company sells and owns the goods, and each parcel
clears customs as the shopper's own import. You still need a mainland
company that registers with customs and shares liability, and a bonded or
direct-mail logistics route. You'll probably hire a Tmall Partner (TP) too.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

The question usually comes from a brand that already imports somewhere. In
the US or the EU, someone on the ground files the entry and owns the duty.
China has that route too. It's called general trade (一般贸易): a Chinese
importer declares the goods, pays full duty and VAT, puts on the Chinese
label and holds the product filings, then sells to distributors and stores.

Tmall Global, Alibaba's marketplace for imported goods, runs on the other
route, cross-border retail import. The shopper buys from you abroad.

> In cross-border retail import, the seller is the overseas-registered
> company selling to mainland shoppers and the owner of the goods. The goods
> are supervised as personal-use imports, with no first-import permit,
> registration or filing required.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce and five other agencies (商务部等六部门),
> 商财发〔2018〕486号, November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

So the job an importer of record does in general trade gets split across
several companies on Tmall Global.

## Tmall Global importer of record duties: who holds each one

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Overseas selling entity | Required. It opens the store and owns the stock | Optional. It sells to the importer | Your own company outside mainland China | 50,000 deposit, refundable, plus a 30,000 annual fee |
| Mainland agent | Required by customs. It registers, declares and shares liability | Not used | The TP or the bonded warehouse, sometimes through its customs broker | 0 in our default, since the TP or warehouse covers it |

Cash column: TheChinaPath calculator data, October 2026, packaged-food

The overseas entity is the one fixed piece. Tmall Global won't take anything
else.

> Merchants must be companies with qualifications outside mainland China and
> an overseas registered trademark. The store entity must be a company
> overseas or in Hong Kong, Macau or Taiwan, and the merchant must give
> Tmall Global the identity and details of any agent operator running the
> store.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), Merchant Acceptance Standards
> (天猫国际招商标准), in force March 2025.
> https://rule.tmall.hk/?type=detail&ruleId=11003210&cId=624#/rule/detail?ruleId=11003210&cId=624

## The mainland agent is the closest thing to an importer of record

This is the role buyers miss. The seller can stay offshore. Customs still
wants a mainland name on the file.

> A cross-border retail import seller must appoint a company registered in
> mainland China. That company registers with customs, is responsible for
> truthful declaration, accepts supervision and bears joint civil liability
> with the seller.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce and five other agencies (商务部等六部门),
> 商财发〔2018〕486号, article 4, November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

In practice it rarely costs a separate fee. Our
[Tmall Global calculator](/tools/tmall-global-setup-and-run) carries a line
for a clearance agent and leaves it at zero by default, because on the
bonded route the TP or the warehouse usually takes the job. Read the
contract anyway. Joint liability means that company answers for your
declarations, so it will check your product data before it files, and
you'll want to know who holds the customs registration if you change
partners.

Food is now stricter.

> Since May 27, 2026, Tmall Global stores in food, infant formula and baby
> food, and supplements and tonics must file a mainland company's business
> license and food business license, its legal representative's ID, and a
> cross-border retail import food entrusted-operation agreement. Tmall gives
> food recall duties as the reason.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), notice on the Merchant Qualification
> Standards (天猫国际商家资质标准), May 2026.
> https://rule.tmall.hk/?type=detail&ruleId=20010174&cId=636#/rule/detail?ruleId=20010174&cId=636

That company still isn't an importer of record, and the goods stay yours.
But a food brand now needs a licensed mainland partner on file before the
store opens. Find out which company your TP plans to put there.

## 1210 bonded vs 9610 direct mail

Customs gives the two cross-border routes a code each.

> Cross-border retail imports reach the shopper either as bonded online
> purchases (网购保税进口, customs code 1210) or as direct purchases
> (直购进口, customs code 9610), and the goods must be on the cross-border
> retail import list.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce and five other agencies (商务部等六部门),
> 商财发〔2018〕486号, article 1, November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

Under 1210 you ship stock in bulk to a bonded warehouse in China before
anyone orders. It clears one parcel at a time when a shopper pays, so
delivery is domestic. Under 9610 the order ships from abroad, parcel by
parcel, and nothing sits in China.

The tax is the same on both.

> Within the limits, cross-border retail imports pay 0% duty, and import VAT
> and consumption tax are charged at 70% of the statutory amount.
> Source: Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs and State
> Taxation Administration (财政部 海关总署 税务总局), 财关税〔2016〕18号,
> March 2016. http://www.mof.gov.cn/gp/xxgkml/gss/201603/t20160324_2510682.htm

For most goods that works out to 9.1% (13% VAT times 70%), usually paid by
the shopper at checkout.

Cash isn't. Bonded means money in China up front. Our
calculator puts a 100,000 RMB warehouse deposit and a 300,000 RMB tax
prepayment ahead of the first order, out of 505,000 RMB in total before day
one (about US$71,000 at a 7.1 planning rate), so the warehouse ties up more
than the platform deposit, the annual fee and the store build combined. Then
it charges per order: 17.35 RMB at the defaults (storage, pick and pack, last
mile and a 5% return allowance), or 6.9% of a 250 RMB basket.

TheChinaPath calculator data, October 2026.

Direct mail skips the deposit and puts the wait on the shopper instead (a
parcel from Europe doesn't land overnight). It suits a test, or a long tail
of slow sellers. Pick one route per product
and label it honestly, because Tmall checks.

> A product listed as a mail-declared, overseas direct-mail item that is
> actually shipped from a bonded warehouse in China is deleted, and a store
> caught three times is closed.
> Source: Tmall Global (天猫国际), Market Management Rules
> (天猫国际市场管理规则), in force May 2026.
> https://rule.tmall.hk/?type=detail&ruleId=11004788&cId=628#/rule/detail?ruleId=11004788&cId=628

## When a distributor comes in

A distributor belongs to general trade. It shows up when you want something
cross-border can't do, and there are two common triggers.

The first is a physical shelf. Cross-border stock is meant for single
parcels to shoppers, and the rules keep it there.

> Goods bought through cross-border e-commerce are final goods for the
> shopper's personal use and may not be resold in the domestic market.
> Bonded online-purchase goods are in principle not allowed to run a
> "bonded plus offline pickup" model outside special customs zones. A single
> item priced above the 5,000 RMB per-order limit may still come in alone in
> its order, paying full duty, VAT and consumption tax.
> Source: Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs and State
> Taxation Administration (财政部 海关总署 税务总局), 财关税〔2018〕49号,
> November 2018. https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=65769

So supermarkets and restaurants get their goods through general trade, and
someone has to be the importer of record. Usually it's the distributor
itself, or an importer that sells to it. Budget the time, too: in our own
work, a distributor contract takes three to five months from the first
meeting to a signature.

The second is a product the cross-border route can't carry at all:
anything missing from the cross-border retail import list, or an order that
would take a shopper past the 26,000 RMB annual quota.

Either way, general trade brings the paperwork back. For food, the importer
files with customs and your plant has to be registered. The pack needs a
Chinese label as well.

> Imported prepackaged food must carry a Chinese label stating the origin
> and the name, address and contact of the domestic agent, or it may not be
> imported. Food importers and overseas exporters must file with the
> inspection authority, and overseas food plants must be registered.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce (商务部) policy database, Food Safety Law
> (食品安全法), articles 96 and 97, as amended September 2025.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

Once a distributor owns the stock, it also sets the shelf price. The same
line runs through Tmall stores: a service TP works on your stock, while a
buy-out TP buys it and behaves like a distributor. Our piece on
[service and buy-out Tmall Partners](/insights/tmall-partner-agency-vs-distributor-model)
walks through who sets the price in each.

## Common set-ups, from a test to a shelf

**A brand testing China.** Your overseas company opens the store, sometimes
on direct mail for a short range, and a TP runs it. Keep the first year small
enough to read the data. Check that the TP, or a warehouse it works with,
will act as the mainland agent, and get that in writing.

**A brand ready to commit online.** Same entity, now on the bonded route
with a TP. At the calculator's default target
of 3.5 million RMB in first-year sales, the TP's retainer and commission come
to 770,000 RMB, about 22% of sales, on top of the 400,000 RMB the warehouse
holds (TheChinaPath calculator data, October 2026). Run the calculator at
your own target before you compare quotes.

**A brand that wants shelves as well.** The Tmall Global store keeps
running, and a distributor imports a separate general-trade range for
offline. That's two partners on two customs routes. Write the split into both
contracts by channel so the distributor's territory doesn't reach the
platforms.

Whichever fits, our guide to
[finding a Tmall Partner](/compass/find-a-tmall-partner-in-china) sets out
the checks, and our
[cross-border setup team](/enter-china/cross-border-setup) settles the entity
and the warehouse route, and who signs as mainland agent, before the store
opens.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does Tmall Global need a Chinese importer of record?**

No. Your overseas company is the seller and owns the goods, and each parcel
clears as the shopper's personal import, so no Chinese company imports them.
Customs does require a mainland company to register, declare and share
joint civil liability with you. That's usually your TP or your bonded
warehouse, sometimes through its customs broker.

**Can my Tmall Partner act as my mainland agent?**

Usually, either directly or through the bonded warehouse it works with.
Ask before you sign. That company shares liability for
your declarations, so the contract should say who holds the customs
registration and how it moves to a new partner if you switch.

**Can a distributor sell the stock from my bonded warehouse?**

Plan on no. Goods bought through cross-border retail import are for the
shopper's personal use and may not be resold, and bonded stock in principle
can't serve offline pickup outside special customs zones. A distributor
needs its own general-trade shipment with a Chinese label and an importer of
record.

**Do I need a company in China to open a Tmall Global store?**

No. The store must be opened by a company outside mainland China with an
overseas trademark. Food and supplement stores, baby food included, are the
exception in practice: since May 2026 they also file a mainland company
holding a food business license, under an entrusted-operation agreement,
before the store can open.
