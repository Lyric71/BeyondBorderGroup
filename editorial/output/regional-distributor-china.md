# Regional distributor in China: how to choose

Choose a regional distributor in China when it can prove access to your
category’s buyers. Choose a national partner when it can show who sells
in each region and how those accounts reorder. If it relies on
sub-distributors, map them before signing. Start with the coverage you can
verify, then expand against results.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

Your importer may sell to a wholesaler that supplies another distributor
before the goods reach a shop. Ask which company invoices that shop, and
which salesperson visits when stock stops moving.

Our guide to [finding a distributor in China](/compass/find-a-distributor-in-china)
covers the search. After the first calls, decide how much territory the
partner can support with its existing accounts.

A national lead makes sense when your launch already needs accounts across
regions and that company can document how it serves them.

<!-- SECTION: Compare the operating models -->

## Regional distributor in China or national network?

Start by asking how the proposed coverage works. The contracting company
may supply customers directly, work through regional firms, or combine both. A
brand-managed network means you contract with regional partners yourself.
Decide who will coordinate those businesses before comparing offers.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Decision | National lead partner | Regional specialist | Brand-managed regional network |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reach | Ask for active accounts by region and the company supplying each | Verify the cities and routes it actually serves | Combine documented territories; identify uncovered areas |
| Category focus | Check the named category team and competing lines | Test buyer relationships in your particular channel | Select a different specialist where the channel requires it |
| Price visibility and control limits | Trace downstream rebates; review restrictions with counsel | Direct supply can shorten reporting; same legal limits | Brand compares terms across partners; same legal limits |
| Cost to compare | Buying margin plus any downstream allowances | Buying margin plus local delivery and sales support | Regional terms plus your own coordination costs |
| Best fit | A partner with proven multi-region accounts and working reporting | A concentrated launch where category access is strong | A brand able to manage separate forecasts and disputes |

</div>

These are structures to investigate, not market price bands. Ask each
candidate to quote the same product basket and named account list.

> Anjoy reported 2,290 distributors at the end of 2025, including 294

These food-company examples support asking for a regional breakdown; they
don’t establish coverage needs in other industries. Don’t compare the
regional counts across the companies as if their territories or products
were identical.

## Ask for the accounts behind the map

Request a redacted transaction extract for your category, with a start and
end date. Keep account identifiers consistent between files. Show orders
and credits separately, in units and value. A directory of shop names says
little about whether they still buy.

Agree a lookback that covers the category’s normal reorder cycle. Deduct
returns when judging activity; a large shipment followed by a full credit
shouldn’t count as a successful account.

After agreeing confidentiality terms, choose some accounts yourself for
authorized reference checks. The buyer should identify its supplier and the
person who takes its orders. A recent return can reveal who handles a
problem once the goods arrive. Match those answers to the proposed
operating structure.

Then follow a product through a reorder. Check the ordering account against
the dispatch record and the receiving location. A shipment into another
wholesaler proves an intermediate sale. Ask for the next transaction before
counting those shops as active retail accounts.

For a foodservice product, a wholesaler’s supermarket list may contribute
little to the launch. Ask whether its staff already call on the cafes or
professional kitchens you need. For a product requiring installation, find
out who performs the work after delivery.

Ask how much time the assigned salesperson can give your line alongside
competing products. Have that person explain the proposed call schedule.

Find out who takes over if the salesperson leaves. A buyer relationship
held by one employee needs a handover plan.

## Map sub-distributors before granting territory

The lead partner should identify every business expected to handle your
goods and explain its role. Record the legal entity and its territory,
then follow stock ownership through each handoff so you can see which
company owes payment when goods have moved but the cash hasn’t.

Name the responsible manager.

That person should be able to resolve a missed delivery even when another
company holds the stock.

A call with the proposed regional operator tests whether the relationship
already exists. If the lead partner will appoint that operator only after
you sign, classify the region as planned and price the proposal accordingly.

Your agreement can require notice of downstream appointments and reports
from those businesses. It should also allocate responsibility when a
sub-distributor stops trading. Have counsel draft the obligations so they
bind the lead partner; a promise made by a company that never signs your
contract can be difficult to use.

Agree how aged inventory is counted and paid for when a territory changes
hands, with the handover procedure in the contract. Check the physical stock
before approving a transfer.

A national importer and a local sales team can perform separate jobs.
The brief for [distribution in China](/enter-china/distribution) should
record both roles. Require the candidate’s proposal to identify the
responsibilities it will accept. Verify the proposed importer’s ability to
handle your products separately from the sales network.

## Compare costs at the account level

A regional quote may look expensive because it includes delivery to shops.
A national quote may leave that cost with a downstream business. Request
the calculation through to delivery at the same customer. Identify allowances
already included in another party’s quote so you don’t count them twice.

Separate the purchase price from promotional allowances. Unsold stock needs
an owner, and the next shipment needs funding while earlier invoices remain
unpaid. For chilled goods, compare quotes only after the storage and
delivery conditions are clear.

Use a common planning period and report the total support spend alongside
the number of accounts that reordered. Define a reorder before running the
comparison: another shipment to the same wholesaler may tell you little
about purchases further down the chain.

Your team will spend time on this. Several regional partners mean separate
forecasts to reconcile and more people to pay or chase. Put staff time into
the comparison, including travel, before deciding that removing a national
lead saves money.

Choose a first territory you can observe and the partner can serve
profitably at your expected volume. Set written volume and service targets,
then check results before expanding. Put the review date in the agreement,
with a defined process for changing the grant if the
results fall short.

Require the evidence used for expansion to identify receiving accounts and
net purchases. A larger forecast alone shouldn’t trigger a wider grant.

## Separate online rights from regional coverage

Write down whether the regional partner may sell online. Name each approved
store and the legal entity holding its account. Agree how
orders outside its offline territory will be handled.
Record any accounts the brand keeps, including national retail customers.
Resolve overlaps before the same buyer receives competing proposals.

Our published [DaVinci Gourmet case](/work/kerry-davinci) records
cross-channel pricing disputes resolved within three months while we ran
its Tmall and JD stores alongside the distributor network. That is a
TheChinaPath project result, checked October 2026, not a promised timetable
for another brand.

The case reports the outcome, not a legal assessment of each pricing term.
Use it as a reason to investigate channel conflict before appointment.

Price visibility does not give a supplier unrestricted control over resale
prices. Keep the operating report separate from any clause that penalizes
a distributor for discounting.

> Article 18 prohibits agreements fixing resale prices or setting minimum
> resale prices, subject to the law’s exceptions and conditions.
> Source: State Administration for Market Regulation (市场监管总局),
> Anti-Monopoly Law, amended June 2022, text posted November 2022.
> https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2023/art_f0fae9eb3a684fc39e84d89eabfc2caa.html

Have China-qualified counsel review the territorial restrictions too. The
same law separately addresses market division among competitors; don’t
organize an agreement between rival distributors to divide customers.

The [China distributor exclusivity guide](/insights/china-distributor-exclusivity)
sets out how territory and performance reviews fit together. Attach the
account map to the proposed grant so the commercial promise can be checked.

## Questions before choosing a distributor

### Is a regional distributor cheaper?

The quote alone won’t tell you. Compare the costs through to the same
customer, including the work your own team must do. A regional partner
that supplies shops directly may include expenses that a national lead
passes downstream. Ask for a cost breakdown using the same proposed
accounts and products.

### Does national coverage mean direct access to every retailer?

No. Ask the candidate to distinguish its direct accounts from those served
by sub-distributors. Verify recent category transactions and speak with
selected buyers. A retailer’s logo in a presentation does not establish
who holds the account, whether it is active, or whether it will list your
product after you sign.

### Can I start in a region and add others later?

Yes, if the agreement leaves that route available. Define the initial
territory and reserve ungranted areas. Set a review date and require
evidence of repeat orders before expansion. Specify how a new territory
would be approved; avoid language that gives national exclusivity
automatically after the first purchase.

### What should a distributor shortlist brief contain?

Name the product range and the buyers you want to reach. State the launch
territory, then explain which channels the brand will retain. Include
storage or service requirements that change who can handle the goods.
Ask candidates for evidence of current active accounts. Record customers
they hope to recruit separately.
