# How LEGO entered China, and what it cost

LEGO began selling in China in 1983 and opened its first mainland brand
flagship in 2016. Its Jiaxing factory expansion carried an announced RMB
1 billion investment, according to The Paper. The sequence separates selling
costs from factory investment. The sources reviewed disclose no total China
retail launch budget, so the factory figure cannot answer what a first store
costs.

> Sources for the opening dates and announced investment: LEGO Group, November 2018 and March 2022; The Paper, June 2025.
> https://www.lego.com/en-gb/aboutus/news/2019/october/steam-courses-for-chinese-schools
> https://news.lego.cn/v/news/article?aid=352
> https://m.thepaper.cn/detail/31041417

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

For a foreign toy brand, the useful question is which commitment comes next.
Selling stock to a retailer creates a different cash obligation from building
a factory to supply a region. LEGO’s chronology helps keep them apart.

Sources and the JD storefront were checked on October 16, 2026. Operating
recommendations below are our analysis, not statements about LEGO’s private
contracts or internal decisions.

<!-- SECTION: Where they are now -->

## What LEGO’s China storefront shows today

The accessible JD shop view separates vehicles from licensed collections. It
also groups learning and play products apart from art and lifestyle themes.
DUPLO appears alongside ranges aimed at older builders.

> The LEGO JD self-operated flagship’s retrieved ranking panel shows six
> product tiles, including DUPLO 10914 and Technic vehicles. It exposes no
> complete assortment total, numeric prices or product review counts.
> Source: JD (京东), storefront observation, October 16, 2026.
> https://lego.jd.com/view_search-396203-0-1-1-24-11.html

That is a partial shop view, with rankings that can change. A rank is not a
unit-sales figure, and the visible tiles cannot establish the size of LEGO’s
China range. Nor can they establish current price positioning.

JD labels this shop as self-operated. That identifies the retail format; it
does not reveal the terms on which JD buys or holds LEGO stock.

We read that navigation as separate routes for a parent buying preschool toys
and someone looking for a display model. For your own store, ask whether a
buyer can identify the right age and use without already knowing the product
name. Check that distinction before buying traffic for a broad catalog.

## How LEGO entered China over several stages

LEGO’s sales history predates the Disneytown store. The table keeps the
production start separate from the factory’s formal inauguration too.

> LEGO dates its first China sales to 1983. In November 2018 it reported
> 47 branded stores in 16 Chinese cities and announced its first regional
> Lunar New Year sets for January 1, 2019.
> Source: LEGO Group, November 2018.
> https://www.lego.com/en-gb/aboutus/news/2019/october/steam-courses-for-chinese-schools

> LEGO’s first mainland brand flagship opened at Shanghai Disneytown on
> May 11, 2016, with 800 square meters of floor space.
> Source: LEGO Group (乐高集团), March 2022.
> https://news.lego.cn/v/news/article?aid=352

> Jiaxing began production at the end of 2015. The expansion announced in
> 2022 added 42,000 square meters and was completed in 2024.
> Source: The Paper (澎湃新闻), June 2025.
> https://m.thepaper.cn/detail/31041417

For a smaller entrant, we suggest an approval condition for each new
commitment, such as a retailer placing a paid repeat order. A new entrant can
test whether retail orders repeat before taking on a larger fixed cost.
Nothing in these sources establishes that another toy brand needs LEGO’s time
frame or factory footprint.

## The earliest evidence records product sales

The earliest verified milestone is product sales. The sources used here do not
identify the first Chinese retail partner. An exact first-channel claim would
go beyond the evidence.

Later disclosures distinguish company flagships from authorized retail.

> In March 2022, LEGO reported seven brand flagships and more than 300
> authorized stores across a retail network covering more than 85 cities
> in China.
> Source: LEGO Group, March 2022.
> https://news.lego.cn/v/news/article?aid=352

Our reading of that network is that stock funding and shop costs need separate
decisions. The store count leaves those costs unexplained. A distributor
proposal should make those obligations explicit before a sales target enters
the agreement.

Use [China distribution planning](/enter-china/distribution) to define
the route, then test a candidate’s actual retail access through the
[China distributor finder](/compass/find-a-distributor-in-china).

Ask a prospective toy distributor which accounts would place the opening
orders, then request evidence of previous replenishment. Mall names alone will
not tell you whether the buyer has placed an order.

Ask to see invoices. Then reconcile them with stock reports.

## What the factory investment actually bought

The disclosed money belongs to manufacturing expansion. Keep that scope in
view when you compare the figure with your own launch budget.

> The Paper reported that LEGO announced RMB 1 billion of additional
> investment in Jiaxing in 2022, adding automated warehousing and
> production facilities. The expansion was completed in 2024.
> Source: The Paper, June 2025.
> https://m.thepaper.cn/detail/31041417

The report gives an announced investment and a completion year. It does not
provide an audited total of cash spent. Using that amount as a store-opening
budget would mix different costs and stages of the business.

> At its November 2016 inauguration, Jiaxing covered about 165,000 square
> meters and employed more than 1,200 people. At opening, LEGO expected it
> to produce 70% to 80% of the products sold in Asia.
> Source: LEGO Group, November 2016.
> https://www.lego.com/en-us/aboutus/news/2019/october/jiaxing-factory-grand-opening

Jiaxing was intended to supply the wider Asian market. Charging all of its
cost to China retail would give you the wrong market comparison. The
production share was an expectation stated at opening; these sources do not
verify the share actually achieved.

Our advice for a smaller entrant is to keep the store budget separate from the
supply decision. Put opening inventory and retailer deductions in the
commercial model. State when you pay the supplier and when the retailer must
pay you. Model local manufacturing only when the order volume and
replenishment needs justify investigating it.

Our first supply question would be: which confirmed orders would a local
source fulfill sooner, and what cash would that require? Inventory funding for
those orders belongs in the working-capital forecast.

Equipment and buildings belong in the capital-spending plan. Keeping separate
records lets you test whether faster replenishment supports the proposed
spending.

A supplier quote leaves customer acquisition and retailer returns unanswered.
Ask for those inputs separately.

<!-- SECTION: Time and setbacks -->

## The expansion did not remove demand risk

The sources give no measure of an avoidable launch delay or lost sales. They
document weak demand after years of investment.

> LEGO’s 2023 results said consumer sales declined in China because of
> the economic environment. The release gave no China decline percentage.
> Source: LEGO Group, March 2024.
> https://www.lego.com/en-gb/aboutus/news/2024/march/lego-delivered-topline-growth-and-outpaced-market-in-2023

That disclosure cannot support a claim that a particular store format failed.
It also cannot put a price on lost time. That statement describes 2023; it
does not establish the results in every later year.

> In August 2026, CEO Niels Christiansen described China as the exception
> to broad first-half growth and said consumers were reluctant to spend.
> The company continued investing in its brand and stores; separate China
> revenue was not disclosed.
> Source: Yicai Global (第一财经), August 2026, reporting the earnings call.
> https://www2.yicaiglobal.com/news/china-is-only-exception-for-legos-broad-base-revenue-growth-in-first-half-ceo-says

We would tie expansion to repeat orders that can be verified. Before approving
another outlet, inspect the stock still sitting in the current one. Track what
happens to the cartons after delivery. Stock that remains on a retailer’s
shelf still affects the next order you hope to receive.

Sell-through means sales to the final buyer. Our proposed reporting rule for a
new partner is a weekly statement of units sold and stock left by product and
outlet. Record transfers between shops separately, and keep returns visible
instead of subtracting them silently from sales. Match it against
replenishment orders before agreeing to another shipment. This is a control
you can negotiate; LEGO’s reporting practices are not disclosed in these
sources.

<!-- SECTION: What to copy and avoid -->

## What a foreign toy brand can use

Keep retail access and supply capacity in separate budgets. For your first
partner agreement, define how sell-through will be reported and when you can
reduce the range. Treat those as proposed controls for your business, not as a
description of LEGO’s contracts.

The regional Lunar New Year products offer another useful example. The
company’s announcement describes products built around a local occasion. Our
inference is that a localized assortment deserves testing at product level,
with a buyer and a reorder decision attached. Agree on the test before
producing the range: which outlets will carry it, and how many units must sell
at the intended price before you repeat the order? Set those measures for your
own launch. No sales lift for LEGO’s regional sets is established by the
launch announcement.

Avoid using flagship counts as a payback calculation. The cited releases do
not disclose China store economics, and the JD snapshot does not fill the gap.
Request the costs and sales evidence for the route you actually intend to
fund.

## Questions about LEGO’s China entry

### Was the flagship LEGO’s first sale in China?

No. LEGO’s own history dates China sales to 1983, while its first mainland
brand flagship opened in 2016. The earlier sales record does not name the
first retailer. A brand can use retail partners before operating its own
flagship, but the sources here do not describe LEGO’s original contract.

### How much did LEGO spend entering China?

The sources reviewed do not give a total entry budget. The RMB 1 billion
figure concerns a later manufacturing expansion announced in 2022. It does not
establish store setup costs, advertising spend or the time it took stores to
repay their costs. Ask a prospective operator to separate inventory funding
from running costs and state which assumptions drive its break-even forecast.

### Does the JD shop prove the China business is profitable?

No. The accessible storefront establishes a retail presence and shows product
groupings. It does not disclose a China profit figure or the cost of acquiring
a customer. Our advice is to assess a proposed route using the partner’s
commercial terms and your own order economics.
