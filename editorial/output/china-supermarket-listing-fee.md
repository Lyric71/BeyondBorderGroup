# China supermarket listing fees: check the charges

Check the documented purpose and legal basis of a China supermarket listing fee.
Some charges are prohibited, including festival fees without promotion services.
Before your distributor commits, agree the promotion budget and who pays it.
Require the retailer’s written terms and proof that the promised work happened
before reimbursing the distributor, net of credits.

<!-- INTRODUCTION -->

“Listing support” can hide very different requests. A buyer may mean a special
display, while the distributor’s budget also includes a charge for opening a
supplier account. Ask for the original Chinese fee names and the retailer’s
documents before discussing the total.

We checked price evidence on October 24, 2026, but could not verify a current
retailer-specific range suitable for this budget. Use a written quote for your
stores and products; old press reports cannot price a launch today. Confirm the
quote’s validity dates and whether each product size or flavor creates a
separate charge.

<!-- SECTION: Fee review -->

## China supermarket listing fee: read each line

The national rules apply to covered retailers and their suppliers; the scope and
payment figures appear below. Use the Chinese terms to match a proposal to its
source. They describe what someone has called the charge, not a guarantee that
it is permitted. The payer column is a proposed contract treatment, not a claim
about what all distributors do.

The barcode row concerns charges from the retailer. It does not cover
registration fees paid to a barcode standards organization.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Fee name | Service or restriction | Charging basis to verify | Price evidence | Payer to agree |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Listing, 进场费 | Ask what specific service it buys; signature-only fees are prohibited under Article 13 | Confirm whether one charge covers every named store | No verified current range | Approve only a lawful, documented charge |
| Barcode, 条码费 | Forced duplicate store codes are prohibited where a compliant barcode works; store-code fees cannot exceed actual cost | Code and documented cost | Actual-cost limit, not a market tariff | Identify the supplier and reject unsupported pass-throughs |
| End-cap or promotional stack, 堆头费 | Define the display space and selling period | Per store and agreed period | Written retailer quote required | Distributor-funded or brand-approved reimbursement |
| Festival, 年节费 | A festival alone does not justify a fee without promotion services | Named campaign and participating stores | Written service quote required | Reimburse only agreed, delivered services |
| Promotion support | Identify the activity and products covered | Fixed fee, or percentage with sales base and returns defined | Written retailer quote required | Allocate separately from product margin |

</div>

> Articles 10–13 require agreed promotional services and restrict signing,
> barcode, and service-free festival charges.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce (商务部), Order 17, October 2006, effective November 15, 2006. https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/dl/file/20211203230882.pdf

MOFCOM’s 2011 clean-up notice also named abusive new-supplier entry and
new-product barcode charges. That historical enforcement document does not
provide a current price list. Renaming a charge “support” gives you no reason to
approve it.

> The clean-up targeted entry and other charges imposed through retailers’
> market advantage; it was not a schedule of permitted fees.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce, December 19, 2011, Notice 485. https://sczxs.mofcom.gov.cn/article/at/cl/201112/20111207899451.shtml

Send disputed wording to the person reviewing the supply contract before any
payment. A distributor’s assurance that every supplier pays the fee doesn’t
explain the service or its legal basis.

<!-- SECTION: What a promotion buys -->

## Specify the display before discussing the price

An end-cap is the display at the end of an aisle. Ask the buyer to mark its
location on a floor plan or photograph. The agreement should identify which
products occupy it and how long they remain there. Record the space dimensions
and confirm the booking with the retailer contact named in the proposal.

The same detail belongs in a booking for a promotional stack elsewhere in the
store. “Premium position” leaves too much room for disagreement when your
distributor sends a picture from a quiet corner near the stockroom.

MOFCOM’s 2015 notice recognizes promotional work such as special displays and
placement on websites or mobile devices. It also says promotional fees should
not replace basic marketing services owed to suppliers, and requires retailers
to disclose charges with their corresponding services.

> The notice calls for disclosed service prices and distinguishes special
> promotional work from basic supplier services.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce, January 26, 2015. https://sczxs.mofcom.gov.cn/article/cbw/cl/201502/20150200885280.shtml

If the proposal includes the retailer’s WeChat mini program storefront, ask
which page carries the product and what the placement dates are. Save the page
or campaign reference alongside the store display brief. A screenshot of a
product being available for sale does not prove that the retailer delivered a
separately purchased promotion.

List each store in the display booking so you know where to check the display.
If a store closes during the campaign, the agreement should explain how the
unused portion is credited.

<!-- SECTION: Funding -->

## Settle who advances the money

Your distribution agreement should say who carries the retailer expense and when
any reimbursement becomes due. The company receiving the retailer’s invoice may
be different from the brand that ultimately funds the promotion. Obtain the
invoice recipient’s legal name as well as the retailer’s contracting entity.

In a buy-out arrangement, the distributor purchases your stock for resale. It
can agree to fund retailer activity from its product margin. Compare the
purchase price with the scope it promises to cover. Uncapped deductions can cut
the cash you receive even when the order price looks acceptable. Write which
expenses are included in that margin and require separate approval for anything
outside the agreed scope.

Another option is a separate brand-funded promotion budget. Set a ceiling and a
written approval process for each campaign. Name the company authorized to
approve changes, including additional stores.

If you share costs, specify the calculation. State whether the agreed percentage
applies to an invoice amount including tax and whether a retailer credit reduces
both parties’ contributions. Document any distributor administration charge
separately from the retailer charge.

Our [distribution service](/enter-china/distribution) covers distributor
sourcing and management. In a selection process, ask each candidate to price the
same account plan so that a lower margin can be compared with the extra costs it
leaves to the brand.

Before choosing a candidate, request a redacted reconciliation from a completed
campaign and trace a retailer invoice through to the stores that received the
service. Other suppliers’ commercial terms can be redacted (you don’t need their
prices), while campaign references must remain checkable.

## Work out what the display must earn

Work out how many units would pay for the display before booking it. The
following is an illustrative planning example, not a retailer quotation or a
market benchmark. Every input is assumed, and the example excludes tax. Use a
consistent tax basis in your own sheet, with returns and delivery costs included
in the unit contribution. Here, contribution means revenue left after the
variable costs of selling each unit, before the display expense.

Suppose you agree to a 2-week display in 4 stores at an assumed RMB 800 per
store per week. The display budget is RMB 6,400. A retailer invoice for 6 stores
would exceed the agreed scope even if its unit rate matched.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Illustrative input or result | Amount | Calculation or meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Participating stores | 4 | Assumed pilot scope |
| Display period, weeks | 2 | Assumed agreed duration |
| Display rate, RMB per store per week | 800 | Assumed input, not a market quote |
| Display budget, RMB | 6,400 | 4 × 2 × 800 |
| Contribution per unit before display cost, RMB | 16 | Assumed after other variable costs |
| Units needed to cover display expense | 400 | 6,400 ÷ 16 |

</div>

> Worked example: TheChinaPath editorial calculation, October 24, 2026.
> Inputs are assumptions. This is not observed retailer or client data.

The 400 units cover only the display expense at that assumed contribution. They
do not cover fixed launch costs. Nor does selling 400 units during the promotion
prove that the display caused those sales.

Compare sales net of returns with a suitable non-promoted period or similar
stores, and check whether stock was available throughout. Treat the comparison
as provisional when you have little history. For a new listing, the first
campaign may mainly tell you whether shoppers will try the product at the agreed
price.

Include the cash timing in the same sheet. A profitable campaign can still
require funding before the retailer pays for the goods. Record payment dates for
the stock and the promotion separately.

<!-- SECTION: Settlement -->

## Read the retailer contract alongside the distribution deal

Once the display budget is agreed, check when the goods account pays.

The national retailer-supplier rules define their scope by retailer size and set
a maximum agreed goods-payment term. These figures are legal parameters, not
estimates of how quickly a retailer will actually pay.

<div class="table-scroll">

| Parameter in the 2006 rules | Figure | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Retailer annual sales threshold | RMB 10 million | Chain-store sales are counted together; check the covered entity |
| Maximum agreed goods-payment period | 60 days after receipt | Match the due date to goods-receipt evidence |

</div>

> Articles 3 and 14 specify the retailer threshold and payment limit.
> Source: Beijing Municipal Government (北京市人民政府), November 15, 2006, official republication marked effective, checked October 24, 2026. https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/qtwj/200611/t20061115_780748.html

Ask the contract reviewer to apply those rules to the actual parties. The
retailer’s obligation to its supplier does not automatically set the
distributor’s payment obligation to your overseas company. Negotiate that second
payment schedule explicitly.

Consignment needs a report that accounts for unsold stock and returns. Ask for
store-level movements matched to payment records. Every deduction should have an
explanation you can check against the agreement before accepting the net
payment.

Keep disputed display charges on a separate reconciliation line while the
parties reconcile the goods account under their contract.

<!-- SECTION: Evidence -->

## Match each invoice to work in the store

Build an evidence file around the retailer’s own campaign reference. Retain the
agreed quote and the invoice. Add dated photographs showing the specified
display in each participating store, plus any relevant digital placement
records.

A photograph taken on opening day establishes very little about the rest of the
campaign. Ask for evidence during the agreed period and record interruptions. A
missing delivery can explain why a display was empty, but you still need to know
who bore that risk under the agreement.

The invoice should also reconcile to the distributor’s claim. If the retailer
granted a credit, the distributor should show it in the same statement. Ask for
proof of payment when the distributor says it has already advanced the money,
with the gross charge and net payment shown separately. The agreement should
also require the distributor to return your share of credits issued after you
reimburse it.

Zhanjiang’s market regulator warned local retailers in July 2026 against
unagreed or unmarked charges and charging without delivering the stated service.
It also warned against arbitrary extensions of payment terms. This is a local
enforcement reminder, not evidence of a new nationwide fee schedule.

> Zhanjiang’s notice calls for clear service charges and action against
> improper fees and payment practices.
> Source: Zhanjiang Administration for Market Regulation (湛江市市场监督管理局), July 9, 2026. https://www.zhanjiang.gov.cn/zdlyxxgk/sypaq/aqxf/content/post_2199715.html

## Questions before approving supermarket fees

### What is a normal listing fee in China?

This review did not verify a current price range that a brand could apply across
supermarket chains. Request a dated quote for the actual store list and
products. Compare the service scope alongside the amount, and separate
prohibited charges from legitimate promotional work before using the quote in a
launch budget.

### Does the distributor have to pay upfront?

The funding arrangement needs to be agreed. Ask whether the distributor absorbs
the expense within its margin, advances it for reimbursement, or requires a
brand-funded campaign account. Then define when funds move and what documents
support the payment. Do not infer an obligation to advance money from the word
“distributor.”

### How do we handle a festival fee?

Ask for the campaign behind it. Specify the products and participating stores,
with dates and the promised activity. A festival name on an invoice gives you
little evidence to assess. Compare the proposed charge with the restrictions
above, and require contract review if its basis remains unclear before
authorizing payment.

### What if the distributor sends only a spreadsheet?

Use the spreadsheet to identify the claims, then request the supporting retailer
documents. Match each line to an approved campaign and reconcile any credits. If
the distributor has grouped several brands together, ask how your share was
calculated. Agree that allocation method before the campaign so a shared invoice
can be checked afterward. Mark disputed lines separately.
