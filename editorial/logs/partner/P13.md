## Brief vs source: a framing correction (SPEC "when to stop", item 4)

The brief's answer line reads the SPC case as a Tmall agency whose fake
orders cost "the brand". The court's text says otherwise, and the draft
follows the court:

- The platform is not named (某平台). The facts (store hosting for merchants
  "in a designated area", 门店托管运营, low-price group-buy vouchers bought
  and redeemed, 核销) point to a local-services group-buy platform, not
  Tmall. The draft never says Tmall or Douyin was the platform.
- The RMB 50,000 deposit was the AGENCY's own service-provider deposit, and
  the RMB 100,000 fine was imposed on the AGENCY. The court says nothing
  about what happened to the merchants.
- The "brand pays" half of the argument therefore rests on separate primary
  sources: the Tmall and Tmall Global merchant agreements (acts through the
  account are the merchant's; deposit deduction; restrictive measures), the
  Taobao sub-account agreement (the seller answers for authorized users),
  Douyin's merchant fake-transaction rule (third-party-run fake orders count
  against the merchant), the Anti-Unfair Competition Law art. 9 para. 1 (the
  operator whose sales are inflated is itself liable) and a 2025 Shanghai
  case where a merchant was fined for 20,000+ fake orders.
- The agency is not named anywhere (the court does not name it). The
  Shanghai merchant fined in 2025 is named by the regulator; the draft
  describes it without its name anyway.
- The brief's lead URL court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/507731.html is the
  People's Court Daily write-up; the SPC release with the full case text is
  507691 (as the P08 run noted). Both cited.
