# Spanish translation brief for Beyond Border Group

Internal reference. Used to keep terminology consistent across `src/pages/es/`.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)

1. **No em dashes (`—`, U+2014).** Replace with comma, period, colon, parentheses, or a hyphen if it's a true hyphenation. This applies to every locale.
2. **No numbered badges in cards.** Never add `01 / 02 / 03`, watermark numbers, or numeric prefixes inside cards, pillars, matrix rows, feature blocks, or any card-like UI.
3. **Brand and product names stay canonical.** Beyond Border Group, BeyondCompass, hubStudio, ChinaWebFoundry, NuvoraStudio, The Red Scroll. Tmall, Tmall Global, JD, JD Worldwide, Douyin, WeChat, RED, Xiaohongshu, Pinduoduo, Taobao, Kuaishou, Weibo, Alipay, Bilibili. Cyril Drouin, Echo Peng, Liyan Ye, Cyril, Echo, Liyan.
4. **Numbers and acronyms stay canonical.** 618, Double 11 (not Doble 11), ICP, KOL, KOC, B2B, DTC, AIGC, OOH, SaaS, GMV, CRM, SEO, SEM, IA.
5. **Diacritics are mandatory in body copy.** `á é í ó ú ñ ü`. Opening `¿` and `¡` are mandatory in questions and exclamations. Slugs strip diacritics; body text keeps them.
6. **Punctuation conventions (Spanish):** opening `¿` and `¡` always; standard double quotes `"..."` are fine; use a comma after introductory clauses; use the comma as decimal separator (1,5 %) and the period as thousands separator (10.000) only in formal/legal contexts; otherwise prefer the natural reading ("10K USD" stays as in English source).
7. **Use the formal `usted` form** for any reader-facing copy. Conversational tone is fine, but it stays formal-ish (this is a B2B agency).

## Two-step process per section

Apply both steps in order, even if step 1 looks fine:

1. **Step 1 — Humanised translation from English.** Convert idioms, restructure sentences, hit Spanish journalistic register (El País / Expansión / Cinco Días, not literal Anglo-Spanish).
2. **Step 2 — Native rewrite in Spanish only.** Do not look back at the English source. Treat step 1 as a draft that is *too familiar / too word-for-word*. Restructure long sentences, swap weak verbs for strong native ones, drop English-shaped relative clauses and possessives, replace noun chains with verbal constructions where Spanish prefers verbs, and vary rhythm the way a Spanish business journalist would.

Avoid AI tells: "no solo... sino también", balanced triads everywhere, "no se trata de X, se trata de Y", em dashes, "fundamentalmente", "en última instancia", "el verdadero pregunta es", "el corazón del asunto", abstract-noun stacks, sycophantic openers, generic upbeat closes.

### Common AI tells to avoid in Spanish

- "no solo X, sino también Y" → restructure as two clauses or with another connector
- "en última instancia" / "fundamentalmente" / "en esencia" → cut, restate the actual point
- "diverso ecosistema" / "vibrante" / "rico panorama" / "se erige como" / "constituye un testimonio" → cut
- triads everywhere ("estrategia, ejecución y resultados") → use them sparingly, not in every sentence
- title case in headings → use sentence case ("Cómo entramos en China", not "Cómo Entramos En China")
- bolded inline-header lists → write as prose or simple unordered list
- emojis → never
- curly smart quotes from the source → keep straight quotes `"..."`
