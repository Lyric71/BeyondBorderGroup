Start on JD Worldwide. Cat and dog food opens there for a US$5,000 deposit,
no annual fee, and 2.5% of each sale plus a 0.9% transaction fee. Tmall
Global takes the same
2.5% but wants 80,000 RMB up front. Break-even sits near 1 million RMB of
year-one sales. Before any of that, China has to register your factory.

Pet food is a factory question before it's a store question. Chinese customs
treats it as animal feed, and imported feed has to come from a plant customs
has already registered. A brand can't apply for that itself, and money
doesn't speed it up.

The prize is real.

> China's urban dog and cat owners spent 312.6 billion RMB in 2025, up 4.1%,
> and food took 53.7% of it.
> Source: CCTV.com (央视网), on the 2026 China Pet Industry White Paper
> (《2026年中国宠物行业白皮书》), January 2026.
> https://business.cctv.com/2026/01/05/ARTIdmv2AWmVeRVodaZYLOvQ260105.shtml

Imports are going the other way. They fell in 2025, and Chinese brands now
crowd the top of the festival charts.

> China imported US$474.1 million of packaged pet food in 2025, down 5.2%
> from 2024, according to China Customs.
> Source: USDA Foreign Agricultural Service, ATO Shanghai, Pet Food Market
> Update 2026 (GAIN CH2026-0046), April 2026.
> https://apps.fas.usda.gov/newgainapi/api/Report/DownloadReportByFileName?fileName=Pet+Food+Market+Update+2026_Shanghai+ATO_China+-+People%27s+Republic+of_CH2026-0046.pdf

> Of the pet brands that passed 10 million RMB in sales on the first day of
> Tmall's Double 11 in 2025, 68% were Chinese.
> Source: Huanqiu.com (环球网), via Sina Finance, November 2025.
> https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2025-11-08/doc-infwsynp1202307.shtml

## The route in: a registered plant first, a store second

Here's the rule that sets your timeline. Under the customs regulation on
imported feed, pet food and chews count as feed. Feed has to come from a
plant on the customs register, and your company can't put itself on it. The
exporting country's own authority reviews the plant and recommends it to
China. Registration then runs five years.

> Imported feed must come from a registered overseas producer, recommended
> to the General Administration of Customs by the exporting country's
> competent authority after its own review. Registration is valid for five
> years. The definition of feed includes 宠物食品及咬胶 (pet food and chews).
> Source: General Administration of Customs (海关总署), Measures for the
> Inspection, Quarantine and Supervision of Imported and Exported Feed and
> Feed Additives, as amended by Decree No. 262, March 2023, arts. 10 to 13
> and 68.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=97519

Your country also needs a pet food protocol with China, and each protocol
spells out the paperwork. When the UK got access in November 2025, every lot needed a
health certificate. The outer packaging needed the plant's name, its
registration number, and Chinese wording saying the goods are bound for
China and are for pets only.

> Pet food exported from the UK must come from plants registered with China
> on the UK's recommendation, carry a health certificate with every lot, and
> show the plant name, its registration number, 输往中华人民共和国 and
> 仅用于宠物食用 on the outer packaging.
> Source: General Administration of Customs Announcement 2025 No. 230
> (海关总署公告2025年第230号), November 2025, via the Ministry of Commerce.
> https://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zcfb/zgdwjjmywg/art/2026/art_2c3ecb1ff8354d818fe48d7d56539092.html

So if your contract manufacturer has never shipped to China, you're waiting
on two governments. No budget shortens that.

Bonded cross-border doesn't change it. Both pet food lines sit on the
cross-border retail import list, but only as bonded stock, so you can't
ship single bags by direct mail from Europe.

> 23091010 (retail-packed dog or cat food, canned) and 23091090 (other
> retail-packed dog or cat food) are on the cross-border retail import list,
> 仅限网购保税商品 (bonded online stock only).
> Source: Ministry of Finance and 12 other departments, Announcement 2019
> No. 96, December 2019.
> http://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/201912/t20191227_3451448.htm

The 2018 cross-border rules waive the first-import registration that general
trade demands. Quarantine is the exception.

> Cross-border retail imports are supervised as personal-use goods without
> first-import permit, registration or filing requirements, except for goods
> from epidemic areas whose import is suspended. Customs applies necessary
> quarantine before sale.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce and five other bodies, 商财发〔2018〕486号,
> November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

Customs reads pet food straight into that exception.

> Customs applies quarantine access control to the countries and products
> allowed to export pet food to China, and imported pet food must come from
> registered overseas producers.
> Source: Chengdu Customs (成都海关), via the Shanghai Cross-border
> E-commerce Association (上海跨境电子商务行业协会), November 2023.
> https://www.scea.co/trends/show/id/2552.html

Tax is the good news. Pet feed sits in China's 9% VAT band, so a
cross-border shopper pays 70% of that: 6.3%, with no duty and no
consumption tax.

> 宠物饲料，属于饲料的征税范围 (pet feed falls within the scope of feed),
> which the VAT Law taxes at 9%.
> Source: Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration (财政部
> 税务总局), Announcement 2026 No. 9, Annex 1, January 2026.
> https://hainan.chinatax.gov.cn/xxgk_6_1/30163239.html

> Within the limits, cross-border retail imports carry a 0% tariff, and
> import VAT and consumption tax are levied at 70% of the statutory amount.
> Source: Ministry of Finance (财政部), 财关税〔2016〕18号, March 2016.
> http://www.mof.gov.cn/gp/xxgkml/gss/201603/t20160324_2510682.htm

The limits are 5,000 RMB an order and 26,000 RMB a shopper a year. Two big
bags fit with room to spare.

> The single-transaction limit for cross-border retail imports is 5,000 RMB
> and the annual limit 26,000 RMB.
> Source: Ministry of Finance, General Administration of Customs and State
> Taxation Administration, 财关税〔2018〕49号, November 2018.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5440499.htm

General trade stacks more on top. A provisional 10% duty in 2026, then 9%
VAT. And complete foods need a product registration from the Ministry of
Agriculture and Rural Affairs, filed through a Chinese agent or a China
office, with verification testing that can take three months. Treats are
exempt.

> Retail-packed dog or cat food (23091010 and 23091090) carries a 15%
> most-favored-nation duty and a 10% provisional rate for 2026.
> Source: State Council Tariff Commission (国务院关税税则委员会),
> Announcement 2025 No. 11, Annex 1, December 2025.
> https://gss.mof.gov.cn/gzdt/zhengcefabu/202512/P020251229510521217364.pdf

> The ministry reviews the dossier within 10 working days, the testing
> laboratory completes verification testing within three months, and the
> import registration certificate is valid for five years.
> Source: Ministry of Agriculture (农业部), Administrative Measures for the
> Registration of Imported Feed and Feed Additives, arts. 9, 10 and 14, as
> amended November 2017.
> https://www.moa.gov.cn/gk/nyncbgzk/gzk/202210/t20221013_6413266.htm

> Imported 宠物配合饲料 (complete pet food) and 宠物添加剂预混合饲料 (pet
> additive premix) need an import registration certificate. Other pet feed,
> treats included, does not.
> Source: Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs (农业农村部), Pet Feed
> Administrative Measures, Announcement No. 20, arts. 2 and 14, republished
> June 2025.
> https://www.moa.gov.cn/ztzl/sqxzjcgs/xzjcbz/202506/t20250630_6475260.htm

The Chinese label for general trade is its own small project. It names the
product type in set words (全价宠物食品 for a complete food, 宠物零食 for a
treat). It also carries the guaranteed analysis, the registration number,
the country of origin in Chinese and the Chinese company selling it.

> Imported complete pet food and premix labels must show the registration
> number matching the import certificate, the producer and site, the
> country of origin in Chinese, and the name, address and contact details of
> the product's registered sales entity in China.
> Source: Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs (农业农村部), Pet Feed
> Labeling Regulations (《宠物饲料标签规定》), arts. 3, 5 and 15, republished
> June 2025.
> https://www.moa.gov.cn/ztzl/sqxzjcgs/xzjcbz/202506/t20250630_6475269.htm

Rules above checked October 2026.

## What one order of kibble actually costs

Start with a real shelf price.

> A 5.4 kg bag of an imported premium cat kibble sold for 502.55 RMB after
> coupons on Tmall Global's own direct store.
> Source: SMZDM (什么值得买), January 2025.
> https://www.smzdm.com/p/139914873/

So take three baskets around that bag: a 1.8 kg bag at 180 RMB, the 5.4 kg
bag at 500, and two of them at 1,000.

| Return handling and the leg back | 130 | 130 | 130 |

| Share of the order value | 12.9% | 6.8% | 5.1% |
| Tax the shopper pays at 6.3% | 11 | 32 | 63 |

> Cost per kept order for three pet food baskets on the per-order defaults
> in the JD Worldwide model (4 RMB pick and pack, 11 RMB last mile, 15 RMB
> return handling, 5% returns). Tmall Global lands within 1 RMB of each
> figure.
> Source: TheChinaPath calculator data, October 2026.

The small bag is the trap. Couriers and the warehouse take 1,630 RMB of
every hundred parcels whatever's in the box, which is 74% of the store cost
on the 180 RMB bag. On the two-bag order it falls to a third.

One honest caveat. Our calculator prices the parcel, not the kilo, at 11 RMB
for the last mile. A 10.8 kg box costs more to deliver than that. Put your
warehouse's real quote for the heavy box into the shipping field before you
believe the right-hand column.

Even so, the shape holds. Pet food wants a big bag and a two-bag offer.

Break-even, at the 500 RMB bag on a 50% gross margin: each kept order leaves
about 216 RMB after store cost. Against a 25,000 RMB store build over three
years and a partner retainer at the calculator's default 35,000 RMB a month,
JD Worldwide needs about 2,000 kept orders. That's roughly 1 million RMB of
year-one sales, near US$140,000, before advertising. Tmall Global's annual
fee pushes it to about 2,130 orders.

## Which platform first

JD Worldwide, for a first year. At every basket in our model it lands within
1 RMB of Tmall Global per order. What it doesn't ask for is 44,500 RMB of
first-year cash: 14,500 RMB more deposit and the 30,000 RMB annual fee.

Two things flip it. If your year-one plan runs to several million RMB, the
annual fee shrinks to a rounding error. At that size we'd put an imported
brand on Tmall Global, because in our experience it's the store a Chinese
distributor pulls up first when you start talking. Go there.

And if you can film in Chinese and have a host, Douyin opens for 500 to 5,000 RMB.
Pet is growing fast there.

> Douyin e-commerce's pet category GMV has risen 95% year on year since the
> start of 2025, and new brands' GMV 75%.
> Source: Douyin E-commerce (抖音电商), 2025 Pet Consumption Trend Report,
> via Paidai (电商派), August 2025.
> https://www.pai.com.cn/p/01k3ga6n37f0qszmxpfc0yr77g

Watch the channel fee, though. If every order on the 500 RMB bag came
through Douyin Mall at 8.1%, store cost would run about 58 RMB an order.

None of it matters if your plant isn't registered. Then no platform is
first.

## Questions we get asked

**Can I sell pet food in China through cross-border without registering my
factory?**

No. Customs applies quarantine access to pet food on every route, so the
product has to come from an approved country and a plant on the customs
register. The 2018 cross-border waiver of first-import registration stops
at quarantine. Your own government recommends the plant to China, and that
step sets your timeline.

**What tax does a Chinese shopper pay on imported pet food?**

6.3% on the cross-border route. Pet feed is taxed at 9% VAT in China, and
cross-border shoppers pay 70% of it, with no duty and no consumption tax. On
a 500 RMB bag that's about 32 RMB. General trade is heavier: a 10%
provisional duty in 2026, then the full 9% VAT.

**Do pet treats need the same paperwork as kibble?**

Partly. Treats count as feed, so the plant still needs customs
registration. But the agriculture ministry's import registration covers
complete foods and premixes only, so treats skip it under general trade.
On JD Worldwide they pay 5% commission against 2.5% for kibble.
