Start on Tmall Global. Footwear costs 110,000 RMB there before you sell a
pair, against 42,600 RMB on JD Worldwide and 100,000 RMB on Douyin
cross-border. All three take 5% of every sale. After that your return rate
decides the year, and shoes come back often enough to eat a thin margin.

Footwear is one of the easier categories to bring into China. It sits on the
cross-border positive list, needs no import license, and ships to the customer
from a bonded warehouse inside China. The hard part starts after the sale.
Fees first, though, because that is what brands ask.

## What the three platforms charge for footwear

<div class="table-scroll">

| Footwear, first year (RMB) | Tmall Global | JD Worldwide | Douyin cross-border |
|---|---|---|---|
| Security deposit, refundable | 50,000 | 35,500 (about US$5,000) | 100,000 |
| Annual platform fee | 60,000 | 7,100 (about US$1,000) | none |
| Commission on each sale | 5% | 5% | 5% |
| Cash in before the first sale | 110,000 | 42,600 | 100,000 |

</div>

> Deposit, annual fee and commission by category for footwear on Tmall
> Global, JD Worldwide and Douyin cross-border.
> Source: TheChinaPath calculator data, September 2026.

Those are the published bands, checked September 2026. A TM trademark or a
multi-brand store raises the Tmall deposit above the flagship figure, and your
partner may quote differently. Argue from the row.

JD quotes both figures in dollars, on a ladder that steps up with cumulative
sales, so the RMB above is the entry rung at 7.1. Douyin charges no annual fee
at all, which makes it look like the cheap door until you get to returns.

None of that includes stock, media, a Tmall Partner retainer or the bonded
stack. Add the bonded warehouse deposit, the tax prepayment and the store
build and a Tmall Global footwear flagship needs about 535,000 RMB wired
before launch, roughly 150,000 of it refundable.

## The route, and the trap inside it

Shoes are on the positive list, which decides what may enter this way at all.

> The list runs to 1,142 eight-digit tariff lines and covers apparel and
> footwear among the consumer goods it admits.
> Source: Cyberspace Administration of China (中央网信办), carrying People's
> Daily, April 2016. https://www.cac.gov.cn/2016-04/08/c_1118561924.htm

The edition in force is the 2019 list as adjusted by Announcement 2022 No. 7
(https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-02/21/content_5674854.htm). Being
on it comes with something most brands underrate.

> Cross-border retail import goods are supervised as goods for personal use,
> and the first-import permit, registration and filing requirements do not
> apply to them.
> Source: MOFCOM and five other bodies, 商财发〔2018〕486号, effective January
> 2019. https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-12/31/content_5437823.htm

No import license. No Chinese label glued to the box. The product page carries
the Chinese information instead, and the customer buys as an importer of one.
The same rule caps them at 5,000 RMB per order and 26,000 RMB a year
(https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=65769), a ceiling
footwear rarely reaches.

Tax is light too.

> Duty on cross-border retail imports is set at 0%, and import VAT and
> consumption tax are levied at 70% of the statutory amount.
> Source: Ministry of Finance (财政部), 财关税〔2016〕18号, March 2016.
> http://www.mof.gov.cn/gp/xxgkml/gss/201603/t20160324_2510682.htm

The general VAT rate has been 13% since April 2019
(https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2019/content_5416183.htm), so a pair of
shoes carries 9.1%. General trade pays the full 13% plus duty, needs a Chinese
label, and falls under the mandatory standards, GB 30585-2024 for children's
shoes since June 1, 2025
(https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=19BBE2758DF1574AE06397BE0A0A5337).

Now the trap. China sizes shoes by foot length in millimeters.

> GB/T 43293-2022 《鞋号》, a modified adoption of ISO 9407:2019, has applied
> since May 1, 2023.
> Source: National Standard Information Public Service Platform
> (全国标准信息公共服务平台), October 2022.
> https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=EB58F4DA9225B2A2E05397BE0A0A7D33

A European 41 is not a number a Chinese shopper carries around. They know 250,
or 255. Paste a conversion chart onto the product page and move on, and
your returns run hot by month two, because the half-size gap between a Spanish
last and an Italian one does not survive a lookup table. Photograph the insole
against a ruler. Publish the inner length per size, in millimeters. That work
shows up in the returns column.

## What one order actually costs

No reliable public figure exists for the average order value of imported
footwear, so the 600 RMB below is an assumption, not a statistic. Change it in
the calculator to your own basket. The rates are the calculator's defaults: 4
RMB to pick and pack, 11 RMB of last-mile shipping, 15 RMB to handle a parcel
that comes back, 1% to Alipay.

|---|---|---|---|
| Orders kept | 95 | 70 | 50 |
| Platform commission at 5% | 2,850 | 2,100 | 1,500 |
| Payment fee at 1% | 570 | 420 | 300 |
| Pick, pack and last mile | 1,500 | 1,500 | 1,500 |
| Return handling and the leg back | 130 | 780 | 1,300 |
| Cost per kept order | 53 | 69 | 92 |
| Share of the 600 RMB order | 8.9% | 11.4% | 15.3% |

</div>

> Cost per kept order for footwear at three return rates, on the per-order
> logistics defaults in the Tmall Global model.
> Source: TheChinaPath calculator data, September 2026.

The calculator ships with a 5% return rate. For shoes that is fiction. The only
dated public number close to this category covers apparel:

> During Double 11 2025 the overall e-commerce return rate was 61.5%, and in
> apparel livestream it ran above 80%.
> Source: Beijing Daily (京报网), carrying CCTV News, January 2026.
> https://news.bjd.com.cn/2026/01/11/11517038.shtml

Plan against that as a ceiling rather than adopting it as your number. Footwear
sits in the same fit-driven bucket and behaves like it, ugly on the first
purchase and better on the repeat.

Breakeven, then. At a 60% gross margin and a 30% return rate, each kept order
contributes 291 RMB. The non-refundable floor on Tmall Global, counting only
the annual fee and the store build, is 85,000 RMB. Call it 292 kept orders,
roughly 417 parcels out the door. Add the retainer and the media budget and
real breakeven sits several times higher.

## Which platform first

Tmall Global, if the brand already has some search demand in China and can fund
the bonded stack. In our experience a flagship there is still the page a
Chinese buyer opens to decide whether a foreign shoe brand is real, and it
outlives the campaign that launched it.

JD Worldwide flips it when you are testing whether China wants the brand at
all. Same 5% commission, a quarter of the cash to get live, a shorter
conversation with your CFO if the answer comes back no.

Douyin cross-border deserves more caution than its price tag suggests. No
annual fee, a refundable deposit, the lowest paper cost of the three, and the
whole exposure sitting in the returns column. Go there once your size data is
clean, not to collect it. The
[Douyin cost calculator](/tools/douyin-cost-calculator) prices that route the
same way.

## Questions we get asked

**Does footwear need a license or a test report to enter China?**

Not on the cross-border route. Goods sold this way are supervised as
personal-use imports, so the first-import permit, registration and filing
requirements do not apply. General trade is a different regime, with the
Chinese label and the mandatory standards.

**What tax does the shopper pay on a pair of imported shoes?**

9.1% for general goods. Duty on the cross-border route is 0% and import VAT is
charged at 70% of the statutory amount, so 70% of 13%. It holds within the
5,000 RMB and 26,000 RMB limits.

**Do I have to convert my size chart to Chinese sizes?**

Yes, and a conversion table is the weak version of doing it. The Chinese
standard measures foot length in millimeters. Publish the inner length for
every size you list, next to your own numbering, and write the fit note for the
market rather than translating yours.
