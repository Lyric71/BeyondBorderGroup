Pick the importer that keeps your factory registration in order and runs the
cold or dry chain your product needs, then check the part that costs most: a
Chinese label that holds up in court. A defective one can cost ten times the
price in damages. Test the importer's label work before you hear its sales
pitch.

China has plenty of food importers, and from across a fair stand they all look
alike.

At a fair, each one says it covers the whole country and clears customs
fast. Few bring a file of the
Chinese labels they've signed off, and that file is the thing to ask for.

Since 2019, customs no longer files a food label before the first shipment.
It inspects labels at the border like any other test item, and the law puts
the importer in charge of getting them right. When a shopper wins a label
claim against a store, the store can recover from whoever was at fault. That
road leads back to the importer, and through your contract, to you.

## Your factory's registration, the importer's paperwork

Customs starts with your factory. Every overseas plant that makes food for
China needs a customs registration, and the rule changed this year.

> Registered overseas food plants mark either their China registration number
> or the number approved by their home authority on the food packaging.
> Registration lasts five years and renews automatically for five more, with
> exceptions. The decree took effect on June 1, 2026 and repealed Decree 248.
> Source: General Administration of Customs (海关总署), Decree No. 280,
> articles 14, 15, 21 and 33, October 2025, published by the China-Africa SPS
> Cooperation Information Network (中国-非洲国家SPS合作信息网).
> https://www.chinaafricasps.org/zh/spsj/zfspaqjgjl/fzshspaqzcpxkj/748006255804485.shtml

Some categories need your government's backing first.

> Seventeen categories need a recommendation from the exporting country's
> authority to register, among them dairy, honey, edible oils, nuts and seeds,
> dried fruit and seasoning powders. Only meat and bird's nest are excluded
> from automatic renewal.
> Source: General Administration of Customs (海关总署), Announcement 2026
> No. 27, March 2026.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202603/content_7063537.htm

Your plant files the application itself. In those categories it attaches the
authority's inspection report and recommendation letter. Plants that still
have to apply for renewal get a longer runway.

> The window to apply for renewal widens from three to six months before
> expiry to three to twelve months.
> Source: General Administration of Customs (海关总署), interpretation of
> Decree 280, carried by the Hunan Provincial Government portal
> (湖南省人民政府门户网站), October 2025.
> http://www.hunan.gov.cn/zqt/zcjd/bmjd/202510/t20251031_33837720.html

A good importer asks for your registration number in the first meeting,
checks it against the list customs publishes and puts your renewal date in its
own calendar. A weak one learns about a lapsed registration when a container
is already sitting at the port.

Then there's the importer's own side of the paperwork.

> Overseas exporters and producers answer for the content of their labels.
> The importer must run a system for checking overseas exporters and
> producers, and may not import what fails it. Food importers file with the
> entry-exit inspection authority.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce policy database (商务部), Food Safety Law of the
> PRC, articles 94 and 96, as amended September 2025.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

Read article 94 from the other side of the table. The law tells the importer
to audit you. An importer that never asks for your specifications or test
reports is skipping a job the law gives it, and the label check is part of
that same job.

## The Chinese label is where the money goes

Customs used to file first-time food labels. It stopped.

> Since October 1, 2019, customs no longer files the labels of first-time
> imported prepackaged food. The importer must check that the Chinese label
> meets Chinese law and the national food safety standards, and may not
> import goods whose label fails.
> Source: General Administration of Customs (海关总署), Announcement 2019
> No. 70, April 2019.
> https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/05/content_5448950.htm

> Imported prepackaged food needs a Chinese label stating the origin and the
> name, address and contact details of the domestic agent, or it may not be
> imported.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce policy database (商务部), Food Safety Law of the
> PRC, article 97, as amended September 2025.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

That domestic agent is usually the importer, so its name goes on every pack
you ship. It has every reason to get the label right. Whether it has the
people to do it is what you're checking.

The cost of getting it wrong sits in article 148.

> A consumer can claim ten times the price paid, or three times the loss,
> from the producer of food that fails food safety standards, or from a
> seller that knew, with a floor of 1,000 RMB. Label defects that neither
> affect food safety nor mislead consumers are exempt.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce policy database (商务部), Food Safety Law of the
> PRC, article 148, as amended September 2025.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

That exemption is narrower than it looks. The Supreme People's Court drew the
line in 2024.

> Courts reject the harmless-defect defense when a label leaves out an item
> the standards require, mislabels one on purpose, or gets one wrong in a way
> that misleads consumers about safety. Typos and formatting slips that
> mislead no one, such as an irregular font size, do not trigger punitive
> damages.
> Source: Supreme People's Court (最高人民法院), 法释〔2024〕9号, articles 6
> and 8, August 2024.
> https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/440841.html

Here's what two missing lines cost a Beijing wine shop.

> A Beijing shopper paid 3,552 RMB for four bottles of imported red wine. The
> Chinese label left out the production date and the distributor's contact
> details, and the distributor it named said it had never sold the wine. The
> Dongcheng District court ordered the shop to refund the price and pay ten
> times the price in damages.
> Source: Beijing Daily (北京日报), March 2025.
> https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s67d2735de4b08edd28f62950.html

Regulators can fine on top of that.

> Selling prepackaged food without a label, or with a label that breaks the
> law, draws a fine of 5,000 to 50,000 RMB when the goods are worth less than
> 10,000 RMB, and five to ten times their value above that.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce policy database (商务部), Food Safety Law of the
> PRC, article 125, as amended September 2025.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

And the standard itself is about to change.

> GB 7718-2025, the new general standard for prepackaged food labels, takes
> effect on March 16, 2027. It bans "zero added," "not added" and "not used"
> claims.
> Source: Beijing Municipal Administration for Market Regulation
> (北京市市场监督管理局), May 2026.
> https://scjgj.beijing.gov.cn/zwxx/scjgdt/202605/t20260507_4639154.html

A European pack that sells on "no added sugar" needs a new Chinese label before
that date. Ask every candidate how it will handle the switch.

What a real label review looks like:

- **A name on it.** One person at the importer checks each label against the
  standard before the first order ships, and signs.
- **The sample, before printing.** You see the Chinese label sample and the
  translation, the same pair customs asks for when it pulls a shipment.
- **Three past labels.** Ask for labels it signed off for other brands, and
  whether any of them drew a complaint. (A good importer has a story about one
  that did.)

## Cold chain or dry chain: ask for the log

For chilled cheese or frozen prawns, the truck matters as much as the label.
China's cold-chain standard sets the numbers.

> During transport, cold-chain food temperature should be monitored
> continuously and recorded at intervals of no more than 10 minutes. Frozen
> food travels at -18°C or colder, chilled food at 0°C to 10°C. At warehouse
> intake and delivery, the full-journey temperature record is checked.
> Source: National Health Commission and State Administration for Market
> Regulation (国家卫生健康委 市场监管总局), GB 31605-2020, in force March 2021,
> copy on the Mawei District Government site (福州市马尾区人民政府).
> https://www.mawei.gov.cn/xjwz/zwgk/zfxxgkzdgz/spypaq/spaqbz/202012/P020201228519962293099.pdf

Ask the importer for the log of one past shipment, from the port to the
warehouse to the store. An importer that runs its own cold chain should have
it on file. One that has to go back to its trucking firm for it is telling you
who really controls the temperature.

Dry goods aren't exempt. The Food Safety Law requires storage and transport
equipment to meet the temperature and humidity the food needs (chocolate in
a Guangzhou summer, say). Ask where your pallets will sit, and whether
that warehouse is the importer's or someone else's.

Then ask for the records.

> Importers must keep records of each import and sale: name, specification,
> quantity, production date, batch number, shelf life, the overseas exporter
> and the buyer.
> Source: Ministry of Commerce policy database (商务部), Food Safety Law of the
> PRC, article 98, as amended September 2025.
> https://policy.mofcom.gov.cn/claw/clawContent.shtml?id=104105

Write a monthly copy of that batch record into the contract, so a recall
doesn't start with guessing which cities got the bad lot.

## Where to meet food importers, and how long it takes

The China Food and Drinks Fair puts thousands of them in one place. Its
spring edition runs in Chengdu.

> The 114th China Food and Drinks Fair, held in Chengdu from March 26 to 28,
> 2026, drew 6,615 exhibitors and more than 410,000 industry visits. The 115th
> fair runs October 15 to 17, 2026 in Nanjing.
> Source: Xinhua (新华网), March 2026.
> http://www.xinhuanet.com/food/20260331/272302f4d9c246caba268dca11a777f4/c.html

Our [guide to China's trade fairs](/insights/china-trade-fairs-find-distributor)
covers which buyers walk which show. Bring your registration number and a
draft Chinese label to the stand. The importers worth signing will start with
those.

Don't plan on signing at the fair. In our own distributor work, a first meeting
to a signed contract runs three to five months (TheChinaPath distributor
sourcing engagements, as published on this site). Meet in March, and expect
to sign in the summer.

Ownership is a separate question. Our piece on [state-owned or private
importers](/insights/china-importer-state-owned-private) covers who pays and
which shelves each can reach.

## Before you sign a food importer

The [guide to finding a distributor in China](/compass/find-a-distributor-in-china)
has the full due diligence list. For food, the contract has to answer a few
questions the general list doesn't.

Who approves each Chinese label, in writing and by name, before it's printed?
Who pays a ten-times claim, and how fast does the importer have to tell you one
has landed? Split that cost between whoever wrote the label content and
whoever approved it.

Then the dull ones, which matter in a recall: a monthly batch and buyer report
in a format you set, and a duty for the importer to track your registration's
renewal date.

Every importer and distributor in [Compass](/compass) has been met in person
and vetted on site. A shortlist usually runs three to five names and takes two
to three weeks from the brief (TheChinaPath, as published on this site). After
signature, [distributor management](/enter-china/distribution) keeps the
reporting going.

## Common questions about food importers in China

**Do I need an importer to sell food in China?**

Only for general trade, the duty-paid route into supermarkets and restaurants.
A cross-border store on Tmall Global sells to shoppers from abroad, so no
Chinese importer takes title. Its food category asks for a 50,000 RMB deposit,
a 30,000 RMB annual fee and a 2% commission (TheChinaPath calculator data,
October 2026). Decree 280 leaves cross-border retail food to separate rules.

**Who pays if a shopper sues over the label?**

The shopper can claim from the producer or the seller. Under article 148, the
business that receives the claim pays first, then recovers from whoever was at
fault. In practice the
claim travels up the chain to the importer and then to you. A contract clause
settles where it stops.

**Does Decree 280 change my existing registration?**

Your registration still lasts five years. From June 1, 2026 it renews
automatically for five more. The exceptions are meat and bird's nest plants,
plants in the middle of a correction order, and countries whose exports
customs has suspended. If you do need to renew, apply three to
twelve months before expiry.

**How much does a food importer take?**

No rule sets the margin. As our distributor guide puts it, the markup depends
on the channel and on who pays for listing fees, marketing and the credit
risk. Compare offers on the shelf price your product would reach, and on what
the importer pays for out of its share.

**Can a trade fair contact become my importer?**

Yes, if it passes the checks. Ask for its importer filing, three labels it
signed off and one temperature log before you meet a second time.
